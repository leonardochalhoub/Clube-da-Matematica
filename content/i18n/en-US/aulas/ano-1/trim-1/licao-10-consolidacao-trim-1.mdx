<Porta nivel="formal" titulo="Trimester roadmap">

This lesson introduces no new content. It is an **integrating workshop** with problems that require combining:

- **Lesson 1**: set notation, intervals, operations on sets
- **Lesson 2**: domain, image, composition, injectivity
- **Lessons 3–4**: linear and quadratic functions
- **Lesson 5**: formal composition and inverse
- **Lessons 6–8**: exponential, logarithm, growth/decay models
- **Lesson 9**: average rate of change

### The trimester's conceptual arc

Trimester 1 builds a single idea from the ground up: **how to describe change**.

$$
\underbrace{\text{Sets}}_{\text{L1}} \to \underbrace{\text{Functions}}_{\text{L2}} \to \underbrace{f(x) = ax+b}_{\text{L3: ARC constant}} \to \underbrace{f(x) = ax^2+bx+c}_{\text{L4: ARC linear}} \to \underbrace{f \circ g,\ f^{-1}}_{\text{L5}} \to \underbrace{a^x,\ \ln x}_{\text{L6-7: ARC proportional to value}} \to \underbrace{N_0 e^{kt}}_{\text{L8}} \to \underbrace{\Delta y/\Delta x}_{\text{L9: gateway to calculus}}
$$

Each step answers the question "what happens to $y$ when $x$ changes a little bit?": linear (always the same), quadratic (grows linearly), exponential (grows proportionally).

<Porta nivel="15" titulo="For a 15-year-old">

**This lesson is the test of what you've learned.** If you understood Trimester 1, you'll solve 70%+ of these problems in 4 hours.

**Mental map of what you should be fluent in:**

1. **Sets and domain**: every function has restrictions. Log requires a positive argument; square root requires a non-negative radicand; denominator cannot be zero.
2. **Function** $=$ a box that receives $x$ and returns $f(x)$. Domain = valid inputs. Image = possible outputs.
3. **Linear** $f(x) = ax + b$: rate of change is **constant** $= a$.
4. **Quadratic** $f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c$: parabola, vertex at $x = -b/(2a)$.
5. **Exponential** $f(x) = a \cdot b^x$: grows/decays **proportional to its own value**.
6. **Logarithm** $\log_b a$: the missing exponent — inverse of exponential.
7. **ARC** $= \Delta y/\Delta x$: the "average velocity" of the function on an interval.

**How to approach combined problems:**

1. **Identify** the type(s) of function involved.
2. **Determine** domain and image based on the physical/real context.
3. **Solve** symbolically.
4. **Check** with a concrete value: substitute an extreme value and see if it makes sense.

<Porta nivel="25" titulo="For an engineering student">

Trimester 1 assembled the **basic vocabulary** of real functions. You now possess the catalogue of elementary functions (polynomial, exponential, logarithmic) and the concept of **average rate of change** — the doorway to derivatives.

### Connection to calculus (Year 2 preview)

The ARC on $[a, b]$ is $(f(b) - f(a))/(b - a)$. When $b \to a$, this becomes the **derivative** $f'(a)$. **This entire lesson is precalculus**: the functions you manipulate are those that will be differentiated and integrated in Year 2.

### Unified diagram (via ARC)

| Family | $f(x)$ | ARC on $[a, b]$ | Behavior |
|---|---|---|---|
| Linear | $ax+b$ | $a$ (constant) | Grows/decreases uniformly |
| Quadratic | $ax^2+bx+c$ | $a(a+b) + b$ — linear in $b$ | Vertex = point of zero ARC |
| Exponential | $c \cdot \alpha^x$ | $c\alpha^a(\alpha^{b-a}-1)/(b-a)$ | Proportional to current value |
| Logarithmic | $\log_\alpha x$ | $\log_\alpha(b/a)/(b-a)$ | Decreases with interval length |

The derivative is the limit of each row of this table as $b - a \to 0$.

### Cross-domain connections

| Domain | Key Trimester 1 function |
|---|---|
| **Physics**: free fall | quadratic $h = h_0 - g t^2/2$ |
| **Finance**: compound interest | exponential $S = S_0 (1+i)^t$ |
| **Biology**: population growth | exponential / logistic |
| **Chemistry**: radioactive half-life | decreasing exponential |
| **Engineering**: Beer-Lambert law | exponential $I = I_0 e^{-\mu x}$ |
| **Acoustics**: decibels | logarithm $L = 10 \log_{10}(P/P_0)$ |

### Rigor of proofs

Proof exercises train:

- **Even/odd decomposition**: $f(x) = [f(x) + f(-x)]/2 + [f(x) - f(-x)]/2$.
- **Optimization without calculus**: completing the square.
- **Substitution $u = 2^x$** to reduce exponential to quadratic.

</Porta>

<Porta nivel="40" titulo="For a senior professional">

From the analyst's perspective, Trimester 1 establishes the blocks of **smooth function space** $C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})$ — closed under sum, product, composition, local inverse (where derivative is nonzero).

### Algebraic structure

- **Polynomials** $\mathbb{R}[x]$: ring — sum and product closed; no multiplicative inverse in general.
- **Rational functions** $\mathbb{R}(x)$: field — quotient of polynomials, defined where denominator is nonzero.
- **Exponential and log** extend to **transcendental functions**: they satisfy no polynomial equation. Liouville (1844) proved that $e^x$ and $\ln x$ do not admit an elementary antiderivative in all contexts.

### Applications in ML / quant finance

- **Logistic regression**: $\sigma(x) = 1/(1 + e^{-x})$ — combines exponential and rational, trains binary classifiers.
- **Black-Scholes**: $C = S \Phi(d_1) - K e^{-rT} \Phi(d_2)$ — option priced via exponential composed with cumulative normal.
- **Gradient decay**: schedulers `exponential_decay` in optimizers (Adam, RMSprop) use $\eta_t = \eta_0 \cdot \gamma^t$.

### What comes next

- **Trimester 2** (Lessons 11–20): trigonometry + sequences — generalization of periodic functions and bridge to limit.
- **Trimester 3** (geometry) and **Trimester 4** (advanced exponential and statistics) — closing the precalculus of high school.
- **Year 2 - Trimester 5**: formal limit $\varepsilon$-$\delta$. **Lesson 41** marks the transition to rigorous analysis.

</Porta>

<Porta nivel="pratica" titulo="Practical application — electric bill, interest, and COVID">

**Three real Brazilian phenomena that use at least three families from Trimester 1.**

### 1. Electric bill (linear + threshold)

ANEEL models the basic invoice as $F(\text{kWh}) = 0.76 \cdot \text{kWh} + 4.68$ (residential rate Green Flag, 2024, in USD equivalent). This is a **linear function**: for each additional kWh, you pay $0.76. The constant term $4.68 is the availability charge (minimum even with zero consumption).

If the bill exceeds a certain threshold, it enters Red Flag with an additional charge — the function becomes **piecewise**.

### 2. Compound interest (exponential + logarithm)

Investment in government inflation-linked bonds earns real rate $r$ per year. If you invest USD 1000 in 2025 with a real rate of 6.5% per year:

$$M(t) = 1000 \cdot (1.065)^t$$

To find how long it takes to double: $\log_{1.065}(2) = \ln 2 / \ln(1.065) \approx 11$ years.

ARC in the first year: $M(1) - M(0) = 65$ USD/year. In the tenth year: $M(11) - M(10) \approx 131$ USD/year. The ARC **doubles** in the same timeframe the capital does — a hallmark of exponential.

### 3. Pandemic growth (exponential + logistic)

In the early months of COVID-19 in Brazil (March–April 2020), cases grew exponentially: $C(t) \approx C_0 \cdot e^{0.18 t}$ (daily rate ~18%).

With vaccination and control measures, the model becomes logistic: $C(t) = K/(1 + A e^{-rt})$ — natural brake. The inflection point (where new cases were highest) corresponds to $C = K/2$ — exactly the calculation of Example 5.

**Recipe for solving any combined problem of this type:**

1. Identify which family dominates each time period.
2. Determine domain and boundary conditions from context.
3. Use logarithm to "extract" the time for exponential targets.
4. Calculate ARC to measure speed of change at each point.

## Solved examples

<Exemplo titulo="Domain of log composition (application)" numero="1">

**Problem:** Determine the maximum domain of $f(x) = \log_2(x^2 - 4)$.

**Strategy:** The argument of the logarithm must be strictly positive. Solve the inequality $x^2 - 4 > 0$ by factoring and sign analysis.

**Solution:**

1. Condition: $x^2 - 4 > 0$, that is, $(x - 2)(x + 2) > 0$.
2. Roots: $x = 2$ and $x = -2$. Parabola opening upward.
3. The inequality is satisfied "outside the roots": $x < -2$ or $x > 2$.
4. Domain: $\text{Dom}(f) = (-\infty, -2) \cup (2, +\infty)$.

**Check:** At $x = 3$: $f(3) = \log_2(5) \approx 2.32$, defined. At $x = 0$: $0^2 - 4 = -4 < 0$ — undefined, consistent with $0 \notin \text{Dom}(f)$. At $x = 2$: $\log_2(0) \to -\infty$ — not in the domain.

**Source.** [OpenStax College Algebra 2e §6.3](https://openstax.org/books/college-algebra-2e/pages/6-3-logarithmic-functions) — CC-BY 4.0 license.
</Exemplo>

<Exemplo titulo="Exponential equation via substitution (intermediate)" numero="2">

**Problem:** Solve $4^x - 5 \cdot 2^x + 4 = 0$.

**Strategy:** Note that $4^x = (2^x)^2$. Substitute $u = 2^x$ (with $u > 0$): the equation becomes quadratic in $u$.

**Solution:**

1. Substitute $u = 2^x$: $u^2 - 5u + 4 = 0$.
2. Factor: $(u - 1)(u - 4) = 0 \Rightarrow u = 1$ or $u = 4$.
3. Return to the variable $x$: $2^x = 1 \Rightarrow x = 0$. $2^x = 4 \Rightarrow x = 2$.
4. Check in the original: $4^0 - 5 \cdot 2^0 + 4 = 1 - 5 + 4 = 0$ ✓. $4^2 - 5 \cdot 2^2 + 4 = 16 - 20 + 4 = 0$ ✓.
5. Answer: $x = 0$ or $x = 2$.

**Check:** Both solutions satisfy the original equation. The substitution $u = 2^x$ is canonical: whenever $a^{2x}$ appears together with $a^x$, substitute $u = a^x$.

**Source.** [Stitz–Zeager Precalculus §6.3](https://www.stitz-zeager.com/szprecalculus07042013.pdf) — CC-BY-NC-SA license.
</Exemplo>

<Exemplo titulo="Piecewise function modeling (intermediate)" numero="3">

**Problem:** A pool is filled in two stages: in the first 2 hours, with constant flow of 500 L/h; afterward, with constant flow of 800 L/h. Model the volume $V(t)$. How long does it take to fill the 6,000 L pool?

**Strategy:** Piecewise function (linear in each part). Calculate $V(2)$ at the end of the first stage; then solve $V(t) = 6\,000$ in the second stage.

**Solution:**

1. Model: $V(t) = 500t$ for the first 2 hours, then $V(t) = 1\,000 + 800(t - 2)$ afterward.
2. At $t = 2$: $V(2) = 1\,000$ L.
3. Remaining: $6\,000 - 1\,000 = 5\,000$ L to fill.
4. Time in stage 2: $5\,000 / 800 = 6.25$ h.
5. Total time: $2 + 6.25 = 8.25$ h.

**Check:** $V(8.25) = 1\,000 + 800 \cdot (8.25 - 2) = 1\,000 + 5\,000 = 6\,000$ L. ✓ Continuity at $t = 2$: both expressions give 1,000 L.

**Source.** [Yoshiwara — Modeling, Functions, and Graphs chap. 2](https://textbooks.aimath.org/textbooks/approved-textbooks/yoshiwara/) — open license.
</Exemplo>

<Exemplo titulo="Maximum profit with quadratic (advanced)" numero="4">

**Problem:** A company has cost $C(q) = 50 + 20 q + 0.5 q^2$ and revenue $R(q) = 60 q$. Profit is $L(q) = R(q) - C(q)$. (a) When is profit zero? (b) What quantity maximizes profit?

**Strategy:** Calculate $L(q)$, find roots for (a) and the vertex of the parabola for (b). Use the quadratic formula and $x_v = -b/(2a)$.

**Solution:**

1. $L(q) = 60 q - (50 + 20 q + 0.5 q^2) = -0.5 q^2 + 40 q - 50$.
2. **(a) Zero profit:** $-0.5 q^2 + 40 q - 50 = 0$. Multiplying by $-2$: $q^2 - 80 q + 100 = 0$. Quadratic formula: $q = (80 \pm \sqrt{6\,000})/2 \approx (80 \pm 77.46)/2$. Roots: $q \approx 1.27$ or $q \approx 78.73$.
3. **(b) Maximum profit:** vertex at $q^* = -40/(2 \cdot (-0.5)) = 40$. Maximum profit: $L(40) = -800 + 1\,600 - 50 = 750$.
4. Answer: (a) break-even at $q \approx 1.27$ and $q \approx 78.73$; (b) maximum profit USD 750 at $q = 40$ units.

**Check:** Parabola opening downward (coefficient $-0.5 < 0$) — vertex is maximum ✓. Average of roots: $(1.27 + 78.73)/2 = 40 = q^*$ ✓.

**Source.** [OpenStax College Algebra 2e §3.2](https://openstax.org/books/college-algebra-2e/pages/3-2-quadratic-functions) — CC-BY 4.0 license.
</Exemplo>

<Exemplo titulo="Logistic model — inflection point (real modeling)" numero="5">

**Problem:** $P(t) = 200/(1 + 9 e^{-0.5 t})$ (logistic model). (a) Calculate $P(0)$. (b) What is the carrying capacity? (c) At what time $t$ does the population reach half the capacity?

**Strategy:** For (a) substitute $t = 0$. For (b) examine $t \to \infty$. For (c) solve $P(t) = 100$.

**Solution:**

1. **(a)** $P(0) = 200/(1 + 9) = 20$. Initial population = **20**.
2. **(b)** When $t \to \infty$: $e^{-0.5 t} \to 0$, so $P(t) \to 200$. **Capacity = 200.**
3. **(c)** $200/(1 + 9 e^{-0.5 t}) = 100 \Rightarrow e^{-0.5 t} = 1/9 \Rightarrow t = 2 \ln 9 \approx 4.39$.

**Check:** At $t = 4.39$: $P = 200/(1 + 9 \cdot 1/9) = 100$ ✓. This is the **inflection point** of the logistic curve — where the ARC is maximum.

**Source.** [Lebl — Notes on Diffy Qs §1.5](https://www.jirka.org/diffyqs/) — CC-BY-SA license.
</Exemplo>
