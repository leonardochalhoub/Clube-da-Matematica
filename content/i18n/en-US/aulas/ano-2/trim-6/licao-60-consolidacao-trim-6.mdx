### Hierarchy of Differentiation Tools

<Definicao titulo="Derivative — Limit Definition">

Let $f$ be defined on an open interval containing $a$. The **derivative of $f$ at $a$** is

$$f'(a) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(a+h) - f(a)}{h}$$

if this limit exists (and is finite). If it exists, we say $f$ is **differentiable** at $a$.

- Geometrically: slope of the tangent line to the graph of $f$ at $(a, f(a))$.
- Physically: instantaneous rate of change of $f$ at time $a$.

</Definicao>

> "The derivative of a function $f$ at a value $a$, denoted $f'(a)$, is defined by the formula $f'(a) = \lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(a+h)-f(a)}{h}$, provided this limit exists." — [Active Calculus, §1.3](https://activecalculus.org/single/sec-1-3-derivative-pt.html)

### Table of Fundamental Derivatives

| $f(x)$ | $f'(x)$ | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| $x^n$ | $n x^{n-1}$ | Power |
| $e^x$ | $e^x$ | Natural Exponential |
| $a^x$ | $a^x \ln a$ | General Exponential |
| $\ln x$ | $1/x$ | Logarithm |
| $\sin x$ | $\cos x$ | Sine |
| $\cos x$ | $-\sin x$ | Cosine |
| $\tan x$ | $\sec^2 x$ | Tangent |
| $\arcsin x$ | $1/\sqrt{1-x^2}$ | Arcsine |
| $\arctan x$ | $1/(1+x^2)$ | Arctangent |

### Operational Rules

<Definicao titulo="Product, Quotient, and Chain Rules">

Let $f, g$ be differentiable. Then:

- **Product (Leibniz):** $(fg)' = f'g + fg'$
- **Quotient:** $\left(\dfrac{f}{g}\right)' = \dfrac{f'g - fg'}{g^2}$ (where $g \neq 0$)
- **Chain:** $(f \circ g)'(x) = f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x)$

</Definicao>

> "The Product Rule states: if $f$ and $g$ are differentiable functions, then $\frac{d}{dx}[f(x)g(x)] = f'(x)g(x) + f(x)g'(x)$." — [OpenStax Calculus Vol. 1, §3.3](https://openstax.org/books/calculus-volume-1/pages/3-3-differentiation-rules)

### Implicit Derivative and Higher-Order Derivatives

<Definicao titulo="Implicit Differentiation">

Given $F(x, y) = 0$ where $y = y(x)$ implicitly, differentiate both sides with respect to $x$, treating $y$ as a function of $x$ (chain rule for each term involving $y$), and solve algebraically for $y'$.

For higher-order derivatives: $f''(x) = (f')'(x)$, $f^{(n)} = (f^{(n-1)})'$.

</Definicao>

### Linearization and Related Rates

<Definicao titulo="Local Linearization">

The **linearization** of $f$ at $a$ is $L(x) = f(a) + f'(a)(x - a)$. Geometrically: the tangent line. Valid as an approximation when $|x - a| \ll 1$.

</Definicao>

<Definicao titulo="Related Rates">

When two quantities $u, v$ depend on time $t$ and are linked by an equation $F(u, v) = 0$, differentiate this equation with respect to $t$ using the chain rule:

$$\frac{dF}{dt} = F_u \frac{du}{dt} + F_v \frac{dv}{dt} = 0$$

Knowing one of $du/dt$ or $dv/dt$, determine the other.

</Definicao>

### Fundamental Theorem of Differentiability

<Teorema titulo="Differentiability Implies Continuity">

If $f$ is differentiable at $a$, then $f$ is continuous at $a$.

**Proof.** $\lim_{x \to a} [f(x) - f(a)] = \lim_{x \to a} \frac{f(x)-f(a)}{x-a} \cdot (x-a) = f'(a) \cdot 0 = 0.$ Thus $\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = f(a)$.

The converse is false: $f(x) = |x|$ is continuous at $0$ but not differentiable. ∎

</Teorema>

> "If $f$ is differentiable at $a$, then $f$ is continuous at $a$." — [Active Calculus, §1.7](https://activecalculus.org/single/sec-1-7-lim-cont-diff.html)

### Pattern Recognition

| Signal in Problem Statement | Technique |
|---|---|
| "Compute $f'(a)$ directly" | Rules + Table |
| "$y = f(\text{composite})$" | Chain Rule |
| "$F(x, y) = 0$, find $y'$" | Implicit Differentiation |
| "$f''$, concavity, inflection points" | Higher-Order Derivatives |
| "Derivative of $\arcsin$, $\arctan$, $\ln$, $e^x$, $a^x$" | Inverse Table |
| "Approximate $f(x)$ near $a$" | Linearization |
| "How fast does $X$ change with time?" | Related Rates |
| "$f$ is differentiable at $a$?" | Check continuity + two-sided limit |

### Systematic Errors to Watch For

- Forgetting the chain rule on the inner argument (the most frequent error).
- Confusing the product and quotient rules.
- Substituting numerical values **before** differentiating in related rates problems.
- Confusing continuity with differentiability.
- Applying $(\arctan u)' = \tfrac{1}{1+u^2}$ without multiplying by $u'$.

### Directions of Generalization

1.  **Multivariable Calculus:** Jacobian $Df: \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^m$, Hessian $\nabla^2 f$, gradient, divergence, curl.

### Mnemonic for Professionals

Every derivative theory answers the same question: "How does $f$ change linearly when the argument changes a little?" The domain, norm, and topology change — the principle is always the same.

## Solved Examples

<Exemplo titulo="Definition via Limit — Direct Calculation">

**Problem.** Use the definition of the derivative to compute $f'(2)$ where $f(x) = x^2 - 3x + 1$.

**Strategy.** Apply $f'(2) = \lim_{h \to 0} \dfrac{f(2+h) - f(2)}{h}$ directly, expand the numerator, and cancel the $h$.

**Solution.**

$$f(2+h) = (2+h)^2 - 3(2+h) + 1 = 4 + 4h + h^2 - 6 - 3h + 1 = -1 + h + h^2$$

$$f(2) = 4 - 6 + 1 = -1$$

$$\frac{f(2+h) - f(2)}{h} = \frac{(-1 + h + h^2) - (-1)}{h} = \frac{h + h^2}{h} = 1 + h$$

$$f'(2) = \lim_{h \to 0}(1 + h) = 1$$

**Check.** By rules: $f'(x) = 2x - 3$, so $f'(2) = 4 - 3 = 1$. Consistent.

**Source.** [Active Calculus — Boelkins, §1.3, Preview Activity 1.3.1](https://activecalculus.org/single/sec-1-3-derivative-pt.html) — CC-BY-NC-SA.

</Exemplo>

<Exemplo titulo="Chain Rule with Double Composition">

**Problem.** Compute $f'(x)$ for $f(x) = \sin^3(2x + 1)$.

**Strategy.** Identify the composition structure: $f = u^3$ where $u = \sin(v)$ and $v = 2x+1$. Apply the chain rule twice.

**Solution.**

$$f(x) = [\sin(2x+1)]^3$$

Let $u = \sin(2x+1)$, then $f = u^3$ and $f' = 3u^2 \cdot u'$.

Computing $u'$ by the chain rule: $u' = \cos(2x+1) \cdot (2x+1)' = 2\cos(2x+1)$.

Therefore:

$$f'(x) = 3\sin^2(2x+1) \cdot 2\cos(2x+1) = 6\sin^2(2x+1)\cos(2x+1)$$

**Check.** At $x = 0$: $f'(0) = 6\sin^2(1)\cos(1) \approx 6(0.708)(0.540) \approx 2.296$. Numerical confirmation: $\dfrac{f(0.001) - f(0)}{0.001} \approx 2.296$. Consistent.

**Source.** [Active Calculus — Boelkins, §2.5, Example 2.5.3](https://activecalculus.org/single/sec-2-5-chain.html) — CC-BY-NC-SA.

</Exemplo>

<Exemplo titulo="Implicit Differentiation — Tangent to a Curve">

**Problem.** Find the equation of the tangent line to the curve $x^3 + y^3 = 9$ at the point $(1, 2)$.

**Strategy.** Differentiate both sides with respect to $x$, treating $y$ as a function of $x$, isolate $y'$, evaluate at $(1, 2)$, and write the line equation.

**Solution.**

Differentiating $x^3 + y^3 = 9$:

$$3x^2 + 3y^2 \cdot y' = 0 \implies y' = -\frac{x^2}{y^2}$$

At $(1, 2)$:

$$y'\big|_{(1,2)} = -\frac{1^2}{2^2} = -\frac{1}{4}$$

Tangent line equation (point-slope form):

$$y - 2 = -\frac{1}{4}(x - 1) \implies y = -\frac{x}{4} + \frac{9}{4}$$

**Check.** The point $(1, 2)$ lies on the curve: $1^3 + 2^3 = 1 + 8 = 9$. The line passes through $(1, 2)$: $-\tfrac{1}{4}(1) + \tfrac{9}{4} = \tfrac{8}{4} = 2$. Correct.

**Source.** [OpenStax Calculus Vol. 1 — §3.8, Example 3.71](https://openstax.org/books/calculus-volume-1/pages/3-8-implicit-differentiation) — CC-BY-NC-SA.

</Exemplo>

<Exemplo titulo="Linearization — Approximating a Cube Root">

**Problem.** Use the linearization of $f(x) = \sqrt[3]{x}$ at $a = 8$ to approximate $\sqrt[3]{8.1}$.

**Strategy.** Compute $L(x) = f(a) + f'(a)(x - a)$ and evaluate at $x = 8.1$.

**Solution.**

$f(x) = x^{1/3}$, so $f'(x) = \dfrac{1}{3}x^{-2/3}$.

At $a = 8$:

$$f(8) = 2, \qquad f'(8) = \frac{1}{3} \cdot 8^{-2/3} = \frac{1}{3} \cdot \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{12}$$

$$L(x) = 2 + \frac{1}{12}(x - 8) \implies \sqrt[3]{8.1} \approx L(8.1) = 2 + \frac{0.1}{12} \approx 2.00833$$

**Check.** Exact value: $8.1^{1/3} \approx 2.00832$. Relative error $< 0.001\%$.

**Source.** [OpenStax Calculus Vol. 1 — §4.2, Example 4.11](https://openstax.org/books/calculus-volume-1/pages/4-2-linear-approximations-and-differentials) — CC-BY-NC-SA.

</Exemplo>

<Exemplo titulo="Related Rates — Spherical Balloon">

**Problem.** The radius of a spherical balloon is increasing at a rate of $3$ cm/s. What is the rate of change of the volume when the radius is $10$ cm?

**Strategy.** Write $V = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3$, differentiate with respect to $t$ using the chain rule, substitute $r = 10$ and $\dfrac{dr}{dt} = 3$.

**Solution.**

$$V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 \implies \frac{dV}{dt} = 4\pi r^2 \cdot \frac{dr}{dt}$$

Substituting $r = 10$ and $\dfrac{dr}{dt} = 3$:

$$\frac{dV}{dt} = 4\pi (10)^2 \cdot 3 = 1200\pi \approx 3769.9 \text{ cm}^3/\text{s}$$

**Check.** Units: $\text{cm}^2 \cdot \text{cm/s} = \text{cm}^3/\text{s}$. Correct. The volume grows very rapidly because the surface area $4\pi r^2 = 400\pi$ is large.

**Source.** [Active Calculus — Boelkins, §3.5, Example 3.5.1](https://activecalculus.org/single/sec-3-5-related-rates.html) — CC-BY-NC-SA.

</Exemplo>

### Block H — Differentiability (60.49–60.50)

<Exercicio
  numero="60.49"
  dificuldade="comprehension"
  opcoes={[
    { texto: "Continuous but not differentiable at $x = 0$: one-sided limits of the difference quotient differ ($-1 \\neq +1$)", correta: true },
    { texto: "Differentiable at $x = 0$ with $f'(0) = 0$" },
    { texto: "Differentiable at $x = 0$ with $f'(0) = 1$" },
    { texto: "Discontinuous at $x = 0$" },
  ]}
  solucao={<><Eq>{`f(x) = |x|`}</Eq> is continuous at 0 (the one-sided limits match <Eq>{`f(0)=0`}</Eq>).   passos={<><ol>
    <li>Left-hand limit: <Eq>{`\\lim_{h\\to0^-}\\frac{|h|}{h} = \\frac{-h}{h} = -1`}</Eq>.</li>
    <li>Right-hand limit: <Eq>{`\\lim_{h\\to0^+}\\frac{|h|}{h} = \\frac{h}{h} = +1`}</Eq>.</li>
    <li>Since <Eq>{`-1 \\neq +1`}</Eq>, the two-sided limit does not exist.</li>
    <li>Therefore <Eq>{`f'(0)`}</Eq> does not exist — <Eq>{`f`}</Eq> is continuous but not differentiable at 0.</li>
    <li><em>Note:</em> the graph of <Eq>{`|x|`}</Eq> has a "corner" at <Eq>{`x=0`}</Eq> — a visual sign of non-differentiability.</li>
  </ol></>}
