<Porta nivel="formal" titulo="Operational properties and the Squeeze Theorem">

Let <Eq>{`\\lim_{x \\to a} f(x) = L`}</Eq> and <Eq>{`\\lim_{x \\to a} g(x) = M`}</Eq>, where <Eq>{`L, M \\in \\mathbb{R}`}</Eq>. The laws below hold for <Eq>{`x \\to a`}</Eq>, <Eq>{`x \\to a^+`}</Eq>, <Eq>{`x \\to a^-`}</Eq>, <Eq>{`x \\to \\pm\\infty`}</Eq>.

### Algebraic limit laws

<Definicao titulo="Limit Laws (Boelkins §1.4 / OpenStax §2.3)">

| Law | Statement | Restriction |
|---|---|---|
| Sum / difference | <Eq>{`\\lim (f \\pm g) = L \\pm M`}</Eq> | none |
| Constant multiple | <Eq>{`\\lim (c \\cdot f) = cL`}</Eq> | <Eq>{`c \\in \\mathbb{R}`}</Eq> |
| Product | <Eq>{`\\lim (f \\cdot g) = L \\cdot M`}</Eq> | none |
| Quotient | <Eq>{`\\lim (f/g) = L/M`}</Eq> | <Eq>{`M \\neq 0`}</Eq> |
| Power | <Eq>{`\\lim f^n = L^n`}</Eq> | <Eq>{`n \\in \\mathbb{N}`}</Eq> |
| Root | <Eq>{`\\lim \\sqrt[n]{f} = \\sqrt[n]{L}`}</Eq> | <Eq>{`L \\geq 0`}</Eq> if <Eq>{`n`}</Eq> even |
| Absolute value | <Eq>{`\\lim \\lvert f \\rvert = \\lvert L \\rvert`}</Eq> | none |

</Definicao>

> "If $\lim_{x\to a} f(x) = L$ and $\lim_{x\to a} g(x) = M$, then $\lim_{x\to a}[f(x) + g(x)] = L + M$." — [OpenStax Calculus Volume 1, §2.3, Theorem 2.4](https://openstax.org/books/calculus-volume-1/pages/2-3-the-limit-laws)

> "If $\lim_{x\to c} f(x) = L$ and $\lim_{x\to c} g(x) = K$, then $\lim_{x\to c}[f(x) \cdot g(x)] = L \cdot K$." — [APEX Calculus, §1.3, Theorem 1.3.1](https://www.apexcalculus.com/)

### Direct substitution property

<Definicao titulo="Direct substitution">

If <Eq>{`f`}</Eq> is continuous at <Eq>{`a`}</Eq> (polynomials, rational functions with nonzero denominator, <Eq>{`\\sin`}</Eq>, <Eq>{`\\cos`}</Eq>, <Eq>{`e^x`}</Eq>, <Eq>{`\\ln x`}</Eq> with <Eq>{`a > 0`}</Eq>), then:

$$\lim_{x \to a} f(x) = f(a).$$

This reduces limit computation to simple point evaluation.

</Definicao>

### Composition

If <Eq>{`\\lim_{x \\to a} g(x) = b`}</Eq> and <Eq>{`f`}</Eq> is continuous at <Eq>{`b`}</Eq>, then:

$$\lim_{x \to a} f(g(x)) = f(b) = f\!\left(\lim_{x \to a} g(x)\right).$$

**Counterexample without continuity.** Take <Eq>{`g(x) = 0`}</Eq> constant and <Eq>{`f`}</Eq> with discontinuity at <Eq>{`0`}</Eq>. Then <Eq>{`\\lim g = 0`}</Eq> but <Eq>{`\\lim f(g(x)) = f(0) \\neq \\lim_{t \\to 0} f(t)`}</Eq>.

### Squeeze Theorem

<Teorema titulo="Squeeze Theorem (Sandwich Theorem)">

If <Eq>{`g(x) \\leq f(x) \\leq h(x)`}</Eq> in some neighborhood of <Eq>{`a`}</Eq> (except possibly at <Eq>{`a`}</Eq>), and

$$\lim_{x \to a} g(x) = L = \lim_{x \to a} h(x),$$

then <Eq>{`\\lim_{x \\to a} f(x) = L`}</Eq>.

</Teorema>

> "If $g(x) \leq f(x) \leq h(x)$ for all $x \neq a$ in an open interval containing $a$ and $\lim_{x\to a} g(x) = L = \lim_{x\to a} h(x)$, then $\lim_{x\to a} f(x) = L$." — [OpenStax Calculus Volume 1, §2.3, Theorem 2.7](https://openstax.org/books/calculus-volume-1/pages/2-3-the-limit-laws)

**Classical application.** For <Eq>{`\\lim_{x \\to 0} x^2 \\sin(1/x) = 0`}</Eq>: note <Eq>{`-1 \\leq \\sin(1/x) \\leq 1`}</Eq>, therefore <Eq>{`-x^2 \\leq x^2\\sin(1/x) \\leq x^2`}</Eq>. Since <Eq>{`\\lim_{x \\to 0}(\\pm x^2) = 0`}</Eq>, the limit is <Eq>{`0`}</Eq>.

<Insight>

**Canceling is not division by zero.** When <Eq>{`x \\to 3`}</Eq> but <Eq>{`x \\neq 3`}</Eq>, the factor <Eq>{`(x - 3)`}</Eq> is never zero during the limit process. Canceling <Eq>{`(x - 3)`}</Eq> from numerator and denominator is perfectly valid — it is ordinary algebra applied for <Eq>{`x \\neq 3`}</Eq>.

</Insight>

<Porta nivel="5" titulo="For a 5-year-old">

Imagine you and a friend are each walking toward a door. You will get there, and your friend will too.

If you both meet in front of the door to go in together, you both arrive at the same place. Limits work the same way: if two things are getting close to a point, when you add them together, the result also gets close to a point. You just add the two points where they arrive.

There is a special trick: if one thing is **trapped between two others** that arrive at the same place, it arrives there too. It is like being in the middle of a line — the two people in front of you and behind you go to the same room, and you go along.

Division has a problem: you cannot divide by zero. So when the bottom goes to zero, the rule for dividing limits does not work and we need another way.

</Porta>

<Porta nivel="10" titulo="For a 10-year-old">

Limits have **good manners** with math operations. If you know where two functions are going, you know where their sum, product, and quotient go too.

**The basic rules:**
- Sum: if <Eq>{`f(x) \\to 3`}</Eq> and <Eq>{`g(x) \\to 5`}</Eq> when <Eq>{`x \\to 2`}</Eq>, then <Eq>{`f(x) + g(x) \\to 3 + 5 = 8`}</Eq>.
- Product: <Eq>{`f(x) \\cdot g(x) \\to 3 \\cdot 5 = 15`}</Eq>.
- Quotient: <Eq>{`f(x)/g(x) \\to 3/5`}</Eq> — **but only if the bottom is not zero**.

**Simple functions are easy.** For polynomials, just substitute directly: <Eq>{`\\lim_{x \\to 2}(x^2 + 1) = 2^2 + 1 = 5`}</Eq>.

**The sandwich trick.** If one function always stays between two others, and those two arrive at the same point, then the middle one arrives there too. Example: <Eq>{`-x^2 \\leq x^2 \\sin(1/x) \\leq x^2`}</Eq>. When <Eq>{`x \\to 0`}</Eq>, both ends go to zero, so the middle one does too.

**Problem:** sometimes substitution gives <Eq>{`0/0`}</Eq> — that means there is something to cancel before computing.

</Porta>

<Porta nivel="15" titulo="For a 15-year-old teen">

This lesson provides the algebraic tools to **calculate limits without going back to the epsilon-delta definition** every time.

### Direct substitution works when...

For continuous functions (polynomials, sines, exponentials, logarithms with positive domain), just substitute:

$$\lim_{x \to 3} (x^2 - 2x + 1) = 3^2 - 2 \cdot 3 + 1 = 4.$$

### When it gives $0/0$: factor and cancel

Consider <Eq>{`\\lim_{x \\to 3} \\dfrac{x^2 - 9}{x - 3}`}</Eq>. Substitution: <Eq>{`\\dfrac{9 - 9}{3 - 3} = \\dfrac{0}{0}`}</Eq> — indeterminate.

Factor: <Eq>{`\\dfrac{(x-3)(x+3)}{x-3} = x + 3`}</Eq>. Now substitute: <Eq>{`3 + 3 = 6`}</Eq>. **Answer: 6.**

The key: when <Eq>{`x \\to 3`}</Eq> but <Eq>{`x \\neq 3`}</Eq>, we can cancel the factor <Eq>{`(x - 3)`}</Eq> from numerator and denominator without issue.

### When there are roots: multiply by the conjugate

<Eq>{`\\lim_{x \\to 0} \\dfrac{\\sqrt{x+4} - 2}{x}`}</Eq>: substitution gives <Eq>{`0/0`}</Eq>. Multiply by <Eq>{`\\dfrac{\\sqrt{x+4}+2}{\\sqrt{x+4}+2}`}</Eq>:

$$\frac{(x+4) - 4}{x(\sqrt{x+4}+2)} = \frac{x}{x(\sqrt{x+4}+2)} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{x+4}+2} \to \frac{1}{4}.$$

### The Squeeze Theorem

When you cannot calculate directly, squeeze the function between two that you know:

$$\lim_{x \to 0} x^2 \sin(1/x) = 0 \quad \text{because} \quad -x^2 \leq x^2 \sin(1/x) \leq x^2.$$

</Porta>

<Porta nivel="25" titulo="For an engineering student">

Algebraic properties reduce limit computation to **algebra** — you manipulate expressions without redoing the epsilon-delta proof for each problem. But understanding the proofs helps you know when the properties **fail**.

### Proof: sum law

Given <Eq>{`\\lim_{x \\to a} f = L`}</Eq> and <Eq>{`\\lim_{x \\to a} g = M`}</Eq>. For any <Eq>{`\\varepsilon > 0`}</Eq>:
- There exists <Eq>{`\\delta_1 > 0`}</Eq> such that <Eq>{`|f(x) - L| < \\varepsilon/2`}</Eq> when <Eq>{`0 < |x - a| < \\delta_1`}</Eq>.
- There exists <Eq>{`\\delta_2 > 0`}</Eq> such that <Eq>{`|g(x) - M| < \\varepsilon/2`}</Eq> when <Eq>{`0 < |x - a| < \\delta_2`}</Eq>.
- Take <Eq>{`\\delta = \\min(\\delta_1, \\delta_2)`}</Eq>. By the triangle inequality:

$$|(f + g) - (L + M)| \leq |f - L| + |g - M| < \frac{\varepsilon}{2} + \frac{\varepsilon}{2} = \varepsilon. \quad \blacksquare$$

### Proof: Squeeze Theorem

If <Eq>{`g(x) \\leq f(x) \\leq h(x)`}</Eq> and <Eq>{`\\lim g = \\lim h = L`}</Eq>: given <Eq>{`\\varepsilon > 0`}</Eq>, choose <Eq>{`\\delta > 0`}</Eq> such that <Eq>{`|g - L|, |h - L| < \\varepsilon`}</Eq> for <Eq>{`0 < |x - a| < \\delta`}</Eq>. Then:

$$L - \varepsilon < g(x) \leq f(x) \leq h(x) < L + \varepsilon \implies |f(x) - L| < \varepsilon. \quad \blacksquare$$

### Limit of rational functions at infinity

For <Eq>{`\\lim_{x \\to \\infty} \\dfrac{p(x)}{q(x)}`}</Eq> with <Eq>{`\\deg p = m`}</Eq>, <Eq>{`\\deg q = n`}</Eq>: divide numerator and denominator by <Eq>{`x^n`}</Eq>:
- <Eq>{`m < n`}</Eq>: limit is <Eq>{`0`}</Eq>.
- <Eq>{`m = n`}</Eq>: limit is the ratio of leading coefficients.
- <Eq>{`m > n`}</Eq>: limit is <Eq>{`\\pm \\infty`}</Eq>.

### Engineering applications

- **RLC circuits**: transfer function <Eq>{`H(s) = N(s)/D(s)`}</Eq>. DC gain is <Eq>{`H(0) = N(0)/D(0)`}</Eq> by the quotient law (valid if <Eq>{`D(0) \\neq 0`}</Eq>).
- **Numerical analysis**: the truncation error of Euler's method is <Eq>{`O(h)`}</Eq> — the Squeeze Theorem shows the error goes to zero as <Eq>{`h \\to 0`}</Eq>.
- **Signal processing**: <Eq>{`\\lim_{t \\to \\infty} A \\sin(\\omega t)/t = 0`}</Eq> by Squeeze (<Eq>{`-A/t \\leq A\\sin(\\omega t)/t \\leq A/t`}</Eq>).

### Commuting limit with other operations

- Limit commutes with **continuous functions**: <Eq>{`\\lim f(g(x)) = f(\\lim g(x))`}</Eq>.
- Limit commutes with **finite sums**: trivially.
- Limit **does not** commute with **infinite sums** (series) without uniform convergence conditions.
- Limit **does not** commute with **integral** without Lebesgue or dominated convergence.

</Porta>

<Porta nivel="40" titulo="For a senior professional">

Algebraic properties reflect that the operator <Eq>{`\\lim_a`}</Eq> is an **algebra homomorphism** on the space of functions with an existing limit at <Eq>{`a`}</Eq>.

### Algebraic structure

Define <Eq>{`\\mathcal{C}_a = \\{f : \\mathbb{R} \\to \\mathbb{R} \\mid \\lim_{x \\to a} f(x) \\in \\mathbb{R}\\}`}</Eq>. Then:
- <Eq>{`\\mathcal{C}_a`}</Eq> is a **commutative algebra** over <Eq>{`\\mathbb{R}`}</Eq>: closed under sum, product, and scalar multiplication.
- The functional <Eq>{`\\Lambda_a : \\mathcal{C}_a \\to \\mathbb{R}`}</Eq>, <Eq>{`\\Lambda_a(f) = \\lim_{x \\to a} f(x)`}</Eq>, is an **algebra homomorphism** (preserves <Eq>{`+`}</Eq>, <Eq>{`\\cdot`}</Eq>, <Eq>{`c \\mapsto cL`}</Eq>).
- The kernel <Eq>{`\\ker \\Lambda_a = \\{f : \\lim_a f = 0\\}`}</Eq> is an **ideal** of <Eq>{`\\mathcal{C}_a`}</Eq>.

The quotient law is a consequence: if <Eq>{`g \\notin \\ker \\Lambda_a`}</Eq>, then <Eq>{`1/g \\in \\mathcal{C}_a`}</Eq> and <Eq>{`\\Lambda_a(1/g) = 1/M`}</Eq>.

### Squeeze Theorem: topological generalization

In topological spaces <Eq>{`(X, \\tau)`}</Eq>, if <Eq>{`(x_\\alpha)`}</Eq> is a net with <Eq>{`g(x_\\alpha) \\to L`}</Eq> and <Eq>{`h(x_\\alpha) \\to L`}</Eq> in <Eq>{`\\mathbb{R}`}</Eq>, and <Eq>{`g \\leq f \\leq h`}</Eq> along the net, then <Eq>{`f(x_\\alpha) \\to L`}</Eq>. The ordering of <Eq>{`\\mathbb{R}`}</Eq> is essential — the result does not generalize directly to spaces without order.

### Indeterminate forms and analytic singularities

<Eq>{`0/0`}</Eq> is algebraically the case where numerator and denominator have a common factor that cancels. Properly: numerator and denominator belong to the same ideal <Eq>{`(x - a)^k \\mathcal{O}_a`}</Eq>, where <Eq>{`\\mathcal{O}_a`}</Eq> is the ring of analytic functions at <Eq>{`a`}</Eq>. The order of the singularity (multiplicity of the zero) determines whether the limit exists or is infinite.

### Non-standard analysis

In <Eq>{`^*\\mathbb{R}`}</Eq> (non-standard reals), algebraic properties transfer via the **Transfer Principle**: every first-order sentence true in <Eq>{`\\mathbb{R}`}</Eq> is true in <Eq>{`^*\\mathbb{R}`}</Eq>. The limit of <Eq>{`f`}</Eq> at <Eq>{`a`}</Eq> exists and equals <Eq>{`L`}</Eq> iff <Eq>{`f(x) \\approx L`}</Eq> (infinitely close) for all <Eq>{`x \\approx a`}</Eq>, <Eq>{`x \\neq a`}</Eq>.

### Connection to machine learning

The chain rule (backpropagation) is the limit of a composition: <Eq>{`\\lim_{h \\to 0} \\dfrac{f(g(x+h)) - f(g(x))}{h} = f'(g(x)) \\cdot g'(x)`}</Eq>. Algebraic properties of limits ensure that gradients decompose into products of Jacobians along computational graphs.

</Porta>

<Porta nivel="pratica" titulo="Practical algorithm and real data">

### Step-by-step: how to tackle any limit

**Step 1 — Direct substitution.** Try <Eq>{`f(a)`}</Eq>. If it gives a real number, that is the limit (for continuous functions). Done.

**Step 2 — Identify the indeterminate form.** If it gives <Eq>{`0/0`}</Eq> or <Eq>{`\\infty/\\infty`}</Eq>: continue. If it gives <Eq>{`k/0`}</Eq> with <Eq>{`k \\neq 0`}</Eq>: the limit is <Eq>{`\\pm\\infty`}</Eq> or does not exist — analyze the one-sided limits.

**Step 3 — Algebraic manipulation.**
- <Eq>{`0/0`}</Eq> with polynomials: factor numerator and denominator, cancel the factor <Eq>{`(x - a)`}</Eq>.
- <Eq>{`0/0`}</Eq> with roots: multiply by the conjugate.
- <Eq>{`\\infty/\\infty`}</Eq> in rational functions: divide by the highest degree.

**Step 4 — Squeeze.** If there is <Eq>{`\\sin`}</Eq>, <Eq>{`\\cos`}</Eq>, or any oscillating function multiplied by something going to zero, use <Eq>{`-1 \\leq \\sin(\\cdot) \\leq 1`}</Eq> and apply the Squeeze Theorem.

**Step 5 — Verify with SymPy.**

```python
import sympy as sp
x = sp.symbols('x')
sp.limit((x**2 - 9) / (x - 3), x, 3)    # 6
sp.limit((sp.sqrt(x + 4) - 2) / x, x, 0)  # 1/4
sp.limit(x**2 * sp.sin(1/x), x, 0)      # 0
sp.limit((2*x**2 - 3*x + 1) / (x**2 + 5), x, sp.oo)  # 2
```

### Application: rate of change of accumulated inflation

The IPCA accumulated in 12 months can be modeled locally by <Eq>{`I(t) = 0.6t^2 - 4.8t + 10.5`}</Eq> (percent, <Eq>{`t`}</Eq> in months since January). The instantaneous rate of change of the index at month <Eq>{`t = 4`}</Eq> is:

$$\lim_{\Delta t \to 0} \frac{I(4 + \Delta t) - I(4)}{\Delta t} = I'(4) = 1.2 \cdot 4 - 4.8 = 0.$$

In this model, the minimum of the index occurs at <Eq>{`t = 4`}</Eq> (April). Algebraic properties of limits — not the epsilon-delta definition — allow us to calculate this rate of change with a simple cancellation.

### Common errors

- **Cancel before checking.** The factor <Eq>{`(x - a)`}</Eq> can only be canceled when <Eq>{`x \\neq a`}</Eq> — and that is exactly what "approaching <Eq>{`a`}</Eq>" guarantees.
- **Apply the quotient law when <Eq>{`M = 0`}</Eq>.** If the denominator goes to zero, the law does not hold.
- **Forget to check the root condition.** <Eq>{`\\lim \\sqrt[n]{f} = \\sqrt[n]{L}`}</Eq> requires <Eq>{`L \\geq 0`}</Eq> for <Eq>{`n`}</Eq> even.
- **Confuse bilateral limit with one-sided.** The laws hold for one-sided limits individually, but the bilateral limit requires both one-sided limits to exist and be equal.

</Porta>

## Worked examples

<Exemplo titulo="Direct substitution in a polynomial" numero="1">

**Problem.** Calculate <Eq>{`\\lim_{x \\to 3} (2x^2 - 5x + 1)`}</Eq>.

**Strategy.** Polynomials are continuous everywhere. Apply the direct substitution property.

**Solution.**

1. **Verify that the function is continuous at <Eq>{`x = 3`}</Eq>.** Every polynomial is continuous on <Eq>{`\\mathbb{R}`}</Eq>. The limit is simply the function value at <Eq>{`x = 3`}</Eq>.

2. **Substitute <Eq>{`x = 3`}</Eq>:**

$$\lim_{x \to 3}(2x^2 - 5x + 1) = 2(3)^2 - 5(3) + 1 = 18 - 15 + 1 = 4.$$

3. **Why it works.** By algebraic laws: <Eq>{`\\lim x^2 = 9`}</Eq> (power law), <Eq>{`\\lim 2x^2 = 18`}</Eq> (constant multiple), <Eq>{`\\lim 5x = 15`}</Eq>, <Eq>{`\\lim 1 = 1`}</Eq> (constant). Adding: <Eq>{`18 - 15 + 1 = 4`}</Eq>.

**Verification.** For <Eq>{`x = 3.01`}</Eq>: <Eq>{`2(3.01)^2 - 5(3.01) + 1 = 18.1202 - 15.05 + 1 = 4.0702 \\approx 4`}</Eq>. Consistent.

**Source.** [OpenStax Calculus Volume 1, §2.3](https://openstax.org/books/calculus-volume-1/pages/2-3-the-limit-laws) — CC-BY-NC-SA 4.0.

</Exemplo>

<Exemplo titulo="Indeterminate form 0/0 by factoring" numero="2">

**Problem.** Calculate <Eq>{`\\lim_{x \\to 2} \\dfrac{x^2 - 4}{x - 2}`}</Eq>.

**Strategy.** Direct substitution produces <Eq>{`0/0`}</Eq>. The numerator is a difference of squares — factor and cancel the common factor <Eq>{`(x - 2)`}</Eq>.

**Solution.**

1. **Try direct substitution.** <Eq>{`\\dfrac{2^2 - 4}{2 - 2} = \\dfrac{0}{0}`}</Eq> — indeterminate. Algebraic manipulation is necessary.

2. **Factor the numerator.** <Eq>{`x^2 - 4 = (x - 2)(x + 2)`}</Eq>.

3. **Cancel the common factor.** For <Eq>{`x \\neq 2`}</Eq>:

$$\frac{x^2 - 4}{x - 2} = \frac{(x-2)(x+2)}{x-2} = x + 2.$$

4. **Calculate the limit.** Apply direct substitution:

$$\lim_{x \to 2}(x + 2) = 2 + 2 = 4.$$

**Verification.** For <Eq>{`x = 2.01`}</Eq>: <Eq>{`\\dfrac{(2.01)^2 - 4}{2.01 - 2} = \\dfrac{0.0401}{0.01} = 4.01 \\approx 4`}</Eq>. Consistent.

**Source.** [OpenStax Calculus Volume 1, §2.3, Example 2.17](https://openstax.org/books/calculus-volume-1/pages/2-3-the-limit-laws) — CC-BY-NC-SA 4.0.

</Exemplo>

<Exemplo titulo="Indeterminate form 0/0 by rationalization" numero="3">

**Problem.** Calculate <Eq>{`\\lim_{x \\to 0} \\dfrac{\\sqrt{x + 4} - 2}{x}`}</Eq>.

**Strategy.** Substitution gives <Eq>{`0/0`}</Eq>. Eliminate the root in the numerator by multiplying by the conjugate <Eq>{`(\\sqrt{x+4} + 2)`}</Eq>.

**Solution.**

1. **Try direct substitution.** <Eq>{`\\dfrac{\\sqrt{0 + 4} - 2}{0} = \\dfrac{0}{0}`}</Eq> — indeterminate.

2. **Multiply by the conjugate.** For <Eq>{`x \\neq 0`}</Eq>:

$$\frac{\sqrt{x+4}-2}{x} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{x+4}+2}{\sqrt{x+4}+2} = \frac{(x+4) - 4}{x(\sqrt{x+4}+2)} = \frac{x}{x(\sqrt{x+4}+2)}.$$

3. **Cancel <Eq>{`x`}</Eq>** (valid since <Eq>{`x \\neq 0`}</Eq>):

$$= \frac{1}{\sqrt{x+4}+2}.$$

4. **Apply direct substitution:**

$$\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{1}{\sqrt{x+4}+2} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4}+2} = \frac{1}{4}.$$

**Verification.** For <Eq>{`x = 0.01`}</Eq>: <Eq>{`\\dfrac{\\sqrt{4.01} - 2}{0.01} \\approx \\dfrac{0.0025}{0.01} = 0.25 = \\dfrac{1}{4}`}</Eq>. Consistent.

**Source.** [APEX Calculus, §1.3, Example 1.3.4](https://www.apexcalculus.com/) — CC-BY-NC 4.0.

</Ejemplo>

<Ejemplo titulo="Squeeze Theorem with oscillating function" numero="4">

**Problem.** Prove that <Eq>{`\\lim_{x \\to 0} x^2 \\cos(1/x) = 0`}</Eq>.

**Strategy.** The function <Eq>{`\\cos(1/x)`}</Eq> oscillates indefinitely as <Eq>{`x \\to 0`}</Eq> — we cannot calculate its limit directly. But <Eq>{`|\\cos(1/x)| \\leq 1`}</Eq> always. Use the Squeeze.

**Solution.**

1. **Establish bounds.** For all <Eq>{`x \\neq 0`}</Eq>:

$$-1 \leq \cos(1/x) \leq 1.$$

2. **Multiply by <Eq>{`x^2 \\geq 0`}</Eq>** (does not reverse inequalities):

$$-x^2 \leq x^2 \cos(1/x) \leq x^2.$$

3. **Calculate the limits of the bounds:**

$$\lim_{x \to 0}(-x^2) = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad \lim_{x \to 0} x^2 = 0.$$

4. **Apply the Squeeze Theorem.** Since <Eq>{`f(x) = x^2 \\cos(1/x)`}</Eq> is trapped between <Eq>{`-x^2`}</Eq> and <Eq>{`x^2`}</Eq>, both with limit <Eq>{`0`}</Eq>:

$$\lim_{x \to 0} x^2 \cos(1/x) = 0. \quad \blacksquare$$

**Why Squeeze is necessary.** The product law would require <Eq>{`\\lim_{x \\to 0} \\cos(1/x)`}</Eq> to exist — but it **does not** (oscillates between <Eq>{`-1`}</Eq> and <Eq>{`1`}</Eq>). Squeeze bypasses this difficulty.

**Source.** [APEX Calculus, §1.3, Theorem 1.3.7](https://www.apexcalculus.com/) — CC-BY-NC 4.0.

</Ejemplo>

<Ejemplo titulo="Limit of rational function at infinity" numero="5">

**Problem.** Calculate <Eq>{`\\lim_{x \\to \\infty} \\dfrac{3x^2 - 2x + 1}{x^2 + 5x - 4}`}</Eq>.

**Strategy.** Form <Eq>{`\\infty/\\infty`}</Eq> — the numerator and denominator have the same degree, 2. Divide everything by <Eq>{`x^2`}</Eq> to reveal the leading coefficients.

**Solution.**

1. **Identify the degrees.** Numerator: degree 2. Denominator: degree 2. Form <Eq>{`\\infty/\\infty`}</Eq>.

2. **Divide numerator and denominator by <Eq>{`x^2`}</Eq>:**

$$\frac{3x^2 - 2x + 1}{x^2 + 5x - 4} = \frac{3 - 2/x + 1/x^2}{1 + 5/x - 4/x^2}.$$

3. **Calculate each term.** As <Eq>{`x \\to \\infty`}</Eq>: <Eq>{`1/x \\to 0`}</Eq> and <Eq>{`1/x^2 \\to 0`}</Eq>.

4. **Apply algebraic laws:**

$$\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{3 - 2/x + 1/x^2}{1 + 5/x - 4/x^2} = \frac{3 - 0 + 0}{1 + 0 - 0} = 3.$$

**General result.** When degrees are equal, the limit is the ratio of leading coefficients.

**Source.** [OpenStax Calculus Volume 1, §2.3, Example 2.20](https://openstax.org/books/calculus-volume-1/pages/2-3-the-limit-laws) — CC-BY-NC-SA 4.0.

</Ejemplo>
