<Porta nivel="formal" titulo="Graphical Analysis Pipeline">

### The Eight Formal Steps

<Definicao titulo="Sketching Pipeline via Calculus">

1. **Domain** <Eq>{`D \\subseteq \\mathbb{R}`}</Eq>: where <Eq>{`f`}</Eq> is defined. Identify zeros of the denominator, negative radicands, non-positive logarithms.

2. **Intercepts:** <Eq>{`f(0)`}</Eq> (vertical intercept); roots of <Eq>{`f(x) = 0`}</Eq> (horizontal intercepts, when accessible).

3. **Symmetries:** <Eq>{`f(-x) = f(x)`}</Eq> → even (graph symmetric about the <Eq>{`y`}</Eq>-axis); <Eq>{`f(-x) = -f(x)`}</Eq> → odd (symmetric about the origin); periodicity.

4. **Asymptotes:**
   - **Vertical** at <Eq>{`x = a`}</Eq>: <Eq>{`\\lim_{x \\to a^\\pm} f(x) = \\pm\\infty`}</Eq>.
   - **Horizontal** <Eq>{`y = L`}</Eq>: <Eq>{`\\lim_{x \\to \\pm\\infty} f(x) = L`}</Eq>.
   - **Slant** <Eq>{`y = mx + b`}</Eq>: <Eq>{`m = \\lim_{x \\to \\infty} f(x)/x`}</Eq> and <Eq>{`b = \\lim_{x \\to \\infty}(f(x) - mx)`}</Eq>.

5. **First derivative** <Eq>{`f'`}</Eq>: find critical points. Sign table → intervals of increase (<Eq>{`f' > 0`}</Eq>) and decrease (<Eq>{`f' < 0`}</Eq>), local extrema.

6. **Second derivative** <Eq>{`f''`}</Eq>: find candidates for inflection points (<Eq>{`f'' = 0`}</Eq> or undefined). Sign table → concavity up (<Eq>{`f'' > 0`}</Eq>) or down (<Eq>{`f'' < 0`}</Eq>).

7. **Inflection point:** point <Eq>{`c`}</Eq> where <Eq>{`f''`}</Eq> **changes sign** (concavity switches).

8. **Sketch:** mark intercepts, asymptotes (dashed lines), extrema, and inflections; draw the curve respecting growth and concavity in each interval.

</Definicao>

> "If f'(x) > 0 on an interval, then f is increasing on that interval. If f''(x) > 0, then f is concave up. These two pieces of information, combined with critical and inflection points, give a complete picture of the graph." — [OpenStax Calculus Vol. 1, §4.5](https://openstax.org/books/calculus-volume-1/pages/4-5-derivatives-and-the-shape-of-a-graph)

### Combined Behavior Table

| <Eq>{`f'`}</Eq> | <Eq>{`f''`}</Eq> | Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| <Eq>{`> 0`}</Eq> | <Eq>{`> 0`}</Eq> | increasing, concave up |
| <Eq>{`> 0`}</Eq> | <Eq>{`< 0`}</Eq> | increasing, concave down |
| <Eq>{`< 0`}</Eq> | <Eq>{`> 0`}</Eq> | decreasing, concave up |
| <Eq>{`< 0`}</Eq> | <Eq>{`< 0`}</Eq> | decreasing, concave down |

### Definition of Concavity and Inflection

<Definicao titulo="Concavity">

<Eq>{`f`}</Eq> is **concave up** on <Eq>{`I`}</Eq> if <Eq>{`f''(x) > 0`}</Eq> for all <Eq>{`x \\in I`}</Eq> — the curve lies above any tangent. <Eq>{`f`}</Eq> is **concave down** on <Eq>{`I`}</Eq> if <Eq>{`f''(x) < 0`}</Eq>.

</Definicao>

<Definicao titulo="Inflection Point">

<Eq>{`c`}</Eq> is an **inflection point** of <Eq>{`f`}</Eq> if <Eq>{`f`}</Eq> is continuous at <Eq>{`c`}</Eq> and <Eq>{`f''`}</Eq> changes sign at <Eq>{`c`}</Eq>.

</Definicao>

<Insight>

<Eq>{`f''(c) = 0`}</Eq> is necessary but not sufficient for an inflection point. Example: <Eq>{`f(x) = x^4`}</Eq> has <Eq>{`f''(0) = 0`}</Eq>, but concavity doesn't change at <Eq>{`0`}</Eq> — it's not an inflection point.

</Insight>

<Porta nivel="5" titulo="For a 5-year-old">

Imagine you're drawing a hilly road. The road sometimes goes up, sometimes goes down.

When it goes up, the derivative is positive. When it goes down, it's negative. At the top of the hill, it stops for a moment before going down — that's a special point.

Now look at how the road curves. Sometimes the road **curves upward** (like a bowl) and sometimes it **curves downward** (like a dome). The place where the curve switches is an inflection point.

Before drawing anything, it's smart to figure out these special places. Then you just connect the dots!

</Porta>

<Porta nivel="10" titulo="For a 10-year-old">

To make a good sketch of <Eq>{`f(x)`}</Eq>, follow this list:

1. Where does the function exist? (Domain)
2. Where does it cross the axes? (<Eq>{`f(0)`}</Eq> and where <Eq>{`f(x) = 0`}</Eq>)
3. Does it go to infinity somewhere? (Asymptotes)
4. Where does it **go up** (<Eq>{`f' > 0`}</Eq>) or **go down** (<Eq>{`f' < 0`}</Eq>)?
5. Where does it **curve up** (<Eq>{`f'' > 0`}</Eq>) or **curve down** (<Eq>{`f'' < 0`}</Eq>)?

With these 5 items answered, the drawing practically makes itself!

</Porta>

<Porta nivel="15" titulo="For the High School Student">

**The sketching pipeline** has 8 steps. You can memorize them with the acronym **DISSECF** (Domain, Intercepts, Symmetry, Sign of <Eq>{`f'`}</Eq>, Extrema, Concavity, Inflections, Figure).

**Critical step — the sign table:** divide the real number line at critical and inflection points. In each interval, determine if <Eq>{`f' > 0`}</Eq> or <Eq>{`< 0`}</Eq> and if <Eq>{`f'' > 0`}</Eq> or <Eq>{`< 0`}</Eq>. This completely determines the curve's behavior in each segment.

**Slant asymptote:** occurs when the degree of the numerator equals the degree of the denominator plus 1 in rational functions. Perform polynomial division to find <Eq>{`y = mx + b`}</Eq>.

**Inflection ≠ critical point:** local extrema have <Eq>{`f' = 0`}</Eq>; inflections have <Eq>{`f'' = 0`}</Eq> (with sign change). A point can be both (rare).

</Porta>

<Porta nivel="25" titulo="For the Engineering Student">

Sketching via calculus is the **complete qualitative analysis** of a function, combining:

**Mean Value Theorem (MVT):** it underlies that <Eq>{`f' > 0`}</Eq> implies strict increase. Without MVT, knowing only that <Eq>{`f'(c) > 0`}</Eq> at a point doesn't guarantee increase over an interval.

**Concavity and f'':** if <Eq>{`f''`}</Eq> exists and is positive on <Eq>{`I`}</Eq>, then <Eq>{`f'`}</Eq> is increasing on <Eq>{`I`}</Eq> — the function is "accelerating." In numerical analysis, concavity affects the accuracy of integration methods (trapezoidal rule overestimates when <Eq>{`f'' < 0`}</Eq>).

**Slant asymptotes:** for <Eq>{`f(x) = P(x)/Q(x)`}</Eq> with <Eq>{`\\deg P = \\deg Q + 1`}</Eq>: perform Euclidean division. The quotient is the slant asymptote; the remainder <Eq>{`\\to 0`}</Eq> as <Eq>{`x \\to \\infty`}</Eq>.

**Curves in engineering:** analysis of signals (Bode plots), load-displacement curves in structures, and P-V curves in thermodynamics are direct applications of this qualitative analysis — they replace heavy numerical computation when qualitative behavior suffices.

</Porta>

<Porta nivel="40" titulo="For the Senior Professional">

The German **Kurvendiskussion** (curve discussion), mandatory for the Abitur, is precisely this pipeline — one of the rare topics where German, Japanese, and Singaporean curricula converge in detail. It represents the operational synthesis of single-variable differential calculus.

Historically, Leibniz and Johann Bernoulli already used the second derivative to classify extrema in the 17th century. Modern formalization — including concavity as a global property (not point-wise) — came with Cauchy and Weierstrass in the 19th century, when analysis replaced intuitive geometric argument with rigorous analytical argument.

In quantitative finance, the **convexity** of a bond portfolio is precisely the second derivative of price with respect to interest rate. Risk managers use convexity (positive or negative) to price the curvature of payoffs — directly analogous to the analysis of <Eq>{`f''`}</Eq> in this lesson.

In machine learning, analyzing the loss surface via gradient (<Eq>{`f'`}</Eq>) and Hessian (<Eq>{`f''`}</Eq> in high dimensions) is the direct extension. Saddle points (where the gradient vanishes but it's not a minimum) are the bane of optimizers — an exact analog to <Eq>{`f'(c) = 0`}</Eq> without an extremum, seen in L61.

</Porta>

<Porta nivel="pratica" titulo="Brazilian Practical Application">

**Analysis of an agricultural production curve.** Soybean yield per hectare, as a function of fertilizer applied, shows typical behavior:

<Eq display>{`P(x) = -0{,}01x^3 + 0{,}6x^2 - 2x + 10, \quad x \in [0, 40]`}</Eq>

where <Eq>{`x`}</Eq> is kg of fertilizer per hectare and <Eq>{`P`}</Eq> is sacks per hectare.

<Eq display>{`P'(x) = -0{,}03x^2 + 1{,}2x - 2`}</Eq>

Zeros of <Eq>{`P'`}</Eq>: discriminant <Eq>{`1{,}44 - 0{,}24 = 1{,}20`}</Eq>. Roots: <Eq>{`x \\approx 1{,}84`}</Eq> and <Eq>{`x \\approx 38{,}16`}</Eq>. In the interval <Eq>{`[0, 40]`}</Eq>: production increases from <Eq>{`x \\approx 1{,}84`}</Eq> to <Eq>{`x \\approx 38{,}16`}</Eq>.

<Eq>{`P''(x) = -0{,}06x + 1{,}2 = 0 \\Rightarrow x = 20`}</Eq> — inflection point where the growth rate starts to decrease (diminishing marginal productivity, a fundamental principle of agricultural microeconomics).

**Practical takeaway:** applying up to 38 kg/hectare increases production; beyond that, excess fertilizer starts to harm. The inflection point at 20 kg indicates where marginal productivity is maximized.

</Porta>

## Solved Examples

<Exemplo titulo="Complete Sketch of a Cubic Polynomial">

**Problem.** Sketch the complete graph of <Eq>{`f(x) = x^3 - 3x`}</Eq>.

**Strategy.** Apply the 8 pipeline steps.

**Solution.**

1. **Domain:** <Eq>{`\\mathbb{R}`}</Eq>.
2. **Intercepts:** <Eq>{`f(0) = 0`}</Eq>. Roots: <Eq>{`x(x^2-3) = 0 \\Rightarrow x = 0, \\pm\\sqrt{3}`}</Eq>.
3. **Symmetry:** <Eq>{`f(-x) = -x^3 + 3x = -f(x)`}</Eq> → odd.
4. **Asymptotes:** none (polynomial).
5. **<Eq>{`f'(x) = 3x^2 - 3 = 3(x-1)(x+1)`}</Eq>**. Critical points: <Eq>{`x = \\pm 1`}</Eq>. Increasing on <Eq>{`(-\\infty,-1)`}</Eq> and <Eq>{`(1,\\infty)`}</Eq>; decreasing on <Eq>{`(-1,1)`}</Eq>. Local max <Eq>{`f(-1) = 2`}</Eq>; local min <Eq>{`f(1) = -2`}</Eq>.
6. **<Eq>{`f''(x) = 6x`}</Eq>**. Zero at <Eq>{`x = 0`}</Eq>. Concave down for <Eq>{`x < 0`}</Eq>; up for <Eq>{`x > 0`}</Eq>.
7. **Inflection:** <Eq>{`x = 0`}</Eq> (sign change of <Eq>{`f''`}</Eq>). Point: <Eq>{`(0, 0)`}</Eq>.
8. **Sketch:** S-shaped curve passing through the origin, maximum at <Eq>{`(-1, 2)`}</Eq>, minimum at <Eq>{`(1, -2)`}</Eq>.

**Verification.** By odd symmetry, the graph is symmetric about the origin — the values match.

**Source.** [OpenStax Calculus Vol. 1, §4.5, Example 4.20](https://openstax.org/books/calculus-volume-1/pages/4-5-derivatives-and-the-shape-of-a-graph) · CC-BY-NC-SA

</Exemplo>

<Exemplo titulo="Rational Function with Horizontal Asymptote">

**Problem.** Sketch <Eq>{`f(x) = \\dfrac{x^2}{x^2 + 1}`}</Eq>.

**Strategy.** Identify asymptotes, use <Eq>{`f'`}</Eq> and <Eq>{`f''`}</Eq>.

**Solution.**

1. **Domain:** <Eq>{`\\mathbb{R}`}</Eq> (denominator never zero).
2. **Intercept:** <Eq>{`f(0) = 0`}</Eq>. Only root: <Eq>{`x = 0`}</Eq>.
3. **Symmetry:** <Eq>{`f(-x) = f(x)`}</Eq> → even.
4. **Horizontal asymptote:** <Eq>{`\\lim_{x \\to \\pm\\infty} x^2/(x^2+1) = 1`}</Eq>. Line <Eq>{`y = 1`}</Eq>.
5. **<Eq>{`f'(x) = 2x/(x^2+1)^2`}</Eq>**. Positive for <Eq>{`x > 0`}</Eq>, negative for <Eq>{`x < 0`}</Eq>. Local minimum at <Eq>{`x = 0`}</Eq> (by sign: <Eq>{`f'`}</Eq> changes from <Eq>{`-`}</Eq> to <Eq>{`+`}</Eq>).
6. **<Eq>{`f''(x) = (2(x^2+1)^2 - 2x \\cdot 2(x^2+1)\\cdot 2x)/(x^2+1)^4 = 2(1-3x^2)/(x^2+1)^3`}</Eq>**. Zero: <Eq>{`x = \\pm 1/\\sqrt{3}`}</Eq>.
7. **Inflections at** <Eq>{`x = \\pm 1/\\sqrt{3}`}</Eq>. <Eq>{`f'' > 0`}</Eq> for <Eq>{`|x| < 1/\\sqrt{3}`}</Eq>; <Eq>{`f'' < 0`}</Eq> for <Eq>{`|x| > 1/\\sqrt{3}`}</Eq>.
8. Symmetric curve, starts at 0, increases asymptotically towards 1.

**Verification.** <Eq>{`0 \\leq f(x) < 1`}</Eq> for all <Eq>{`x`}</Eq> — consistent with horizontal asymptote at 1.

**Source.** [APEX Calculus §3.5, Example 3.5.2](https://www.apexcalculus.com/) · CC-BY-NC

</Exemplo>

<Exemplo titulo="Function with Vertical and Horizontal Asymptote">

**Problem.** Sketch <Eq>{`f(x) = \\dfrac{2x}{x - 1}`}</Eq>.

**Strategy.** Identify vertical asymptote (<Eq>{`x = 1`}</Eq>) and horizontal asymptote, then analyze <Eq>{`f'`}</Eq>.

**Solution.**

1. **Domain:** <Eq>{`\\mathbb{R} \\setminus \\{1\\}`}</Eq>.
2. **Intercepts:** <Eq>{`f(0) = 0`}</Eq>; root: <Eq>{`x = 0`}</Eq>.
3. **Vertical asymptote:** <Eq>{`x = 1`}</Eq> (<Eq>{`f \\to \\pm\\infty`}</Eq>).
4. **Horizontal asymptote:** <Eq>{`\\lim_{x \\to \\pm\\infty} 2x/(x-1) = 2`}</Eq>. Line <Eq>{`y = 2`}</Eq>.
5. **<Eq>{`f'(x) = (2(x-1) - 2x)/(x-1)^2 = -2/(x-1)^2 < 0`}</Eq>** for all <Eq>{`x \\neq 1`}</Eq>. Strictly decreasing function on each branch.
6. **<Eq>{`f''(x) = 4/(x-1)^3`}</Eq>**. Positive for <Eq>{`x > 1`}</Eq> (concave up); negative for <Eq>{`x < 1`}</Eq> (concave down).
7. **No inflections** (denominator never zero outside of <Eq>{`x = 1`}</Eq>).
8. Two branches: <Eq>{`x < 1`}</Eq> (decreasing, concave down) and <Eq>{`x > 1`}</Eq> (decreasing, concave up).

**Verification.** <Eq>{`f`}</Eq> never crosses the asymptote <Eq>{`y = 2`}</Eq> (check: <Eq>{`2x/(x-1) = 2 \\Rightarrow 2x = 2x - 2`}</Eq>, impossible).

**Source.** [Active Calculus §3.2, Activity 3.2.3](https://activecalculus.org/single/sec-3-2-families.html) · CC-BY-NC-SA

</Exemplo>

<Exemplo titulo="Inflection Point and Concavity">

**Problem.** Determine the inflection points and intervals of concavity for <Eq>{`f(x) = x^4 - 6x^2`}</Eq>.

**Strategy.** Calculate <Eq>{`f''`}</Eq>, find zeros, check for sign changes.

**Solution.**

<Eq display>{`f'(x) = 4x^3 - 12x, \quad f''(x) = 12x^2 - 12 = 12(x-1)(x+1)`}</Eq>

Zeros of <Eq>{`f''`}</Eq>: <Eq>{`x = \\pm 1`}</Eq>.

- <Eq>{`x < -1`}</Eq>: <Eq>{`f'' > 0`}</Eq> → concave up.
- <Eq>{`-1 < x < 1`}</Eq>: <Eq>{`f'' < 0`}</Eq> → concave down.
- <Eq>{`x > 1`}</Eq>: <Eq>{`f'' > 0`}</Eq> → concave up.

Inflections at <Eq>{`x = -1`}</Eq> and <Eq>{`x = 1`}</Eq>: <Eq>{`f(-1) = 1 - 6 = -5`}</Eq>, <Eq>{`f(1) = -5`}</Eq>.

**Verification.** Sign change test for <Eq>{`f''`}</Eq> confirmed. Note: <Eq>{`f(x) = x^4 - 6x^2`}</Eq> has <Eq>{`f''(0) = -12 < 0`}</Eq> and <Eq>{`f''(2) = 36 > 0`}</Eq>, consistent.

**Source.** [OpenStax Calculus Vol. 1, §4.5, Example 4.22](https://openstax.org/books/calculus-volume-1/pages/4-5-derivatives-and-the-shape-of-a-graph) · CC-BY-NC-SA

</Exemplo>

<Exemplo titulo="Rational Function with Slant Asymptote">

**Problem.** Sketch <Eq>{`f(x) = \\dfrac{x^2 + 1}{x}`}</Eq>.

**Strategy.** Polynomial division to obtain slant asymptote; analyze <Eq>{`f'`}</Eq> and <Eq>{`f''`}</Eq>.

**Solution.**

1. **Domain:** <Eq>{`\\mathbb{R} \\setminus \\{0\\}`}</Eq>.
2. **Intercepts:** <Eq>{`f(x) = 0 \\Rightarrow x^2 + 1 = 0`}</Eq> — no real roots. No intercept at <Eq>{`x = 0`}</Eq> (outside domain).
3. **Symmetry:** <Eq>{`f(-x) = (-x^2 - 1)/(-x) = (x^2+1)/x = f(x)`}</Eq>... wait: <Eq>{`f(-x) = (x^2+1)/(-x) = -f(x)`}</Eq> → odd.
4. **Vertical asymptote:** <Eq>{`x = 0`}</Eq>. Division: <Eq>{`f(x) = x + 1/x`}</Eq> → slant asymptote <Eq>{`y = x`}</Eq>.
5. **<Eq>{`f'(x) = 1 - 1/x^2`}</Eq>**. Zero: <Eq>{`x = \\pm 1`}</Eq>. Local minimum at <Eq>{`x = 1`}</Eq> (value 2); local maximum at <Eq>{`x = -1`}</Eq> (value −2).
6. **<Eq>{`f''(x) = 2/x^3`}</Eq>**. Positive for <Eq>{`x > 0`}</Eq>, negative for <Eq>{`x < 0`}</Eq>. No inflections (discontinuity at 0).
7. **Sketch:** two hyperbolic branches, each approaching <Eq>{`y = x`}</Eq> and <Eq>{`x = 0`}</Eq>.

**Verification.** <Eq>{`f(x) - x = 1/x \\to 0`}</Eq> as <Eq>{`x \\to \\pm\\infty`}</Eq> — slant asymptote confirmed.

**Source.** [APEX Calculus §3.5, Example 3.5.4](https://www.apexcalculus.com/) · CC-BY-NC

</Exemplo>
