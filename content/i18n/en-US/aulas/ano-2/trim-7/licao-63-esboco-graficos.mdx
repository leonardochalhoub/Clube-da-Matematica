### The eight formal steps

<Definicao titulo="Pipeline for sketching via calculus">

1. **Domain** <Eq>{`D \\subseteq \\mathbb{R}`}</Eq>: where <Eq>{`f`}</Eq> is defined. Identify zeros of the denominator, negative radicands, logarithms of non-positive numbers.

2. **Intercepts:** <Eq>{`f(0)`}</Eq> (vertical intercept); roots of <Eq>{`f(x) = 0`}</Eq> (horizontal intercepts, when accessible).

3. **Symmetries:** <Eq>{`f(-x) = f(x)`}</Eq> → even (graph symmetric about the <Eq>{`y`}</Eq> axis); <Eq>{`f(-x) = -f(x)`}</Eq> → odd (symmetric about the origin); periodicity.

4. **Asymptotes:**
   - **Vertical** at <Eq>{`x = a`}</Eq>: <Eq>{`\\lim_{x \\to a^{\\pm}} f(x) = \\pm\\infty`}</Eq>.
   - **Horizontal** <Eq>{`y = L`}</Eq>: <Eq>{`\\lim_{x \\to \\pm\\infty} f(x) = L`}</Eq>.
   - **Oblique** <Eq>{`y = mx + b`}</Eq>: <Eq>{`m = \\lim_{x \\to \\infty} f(x)/x`}</Eq> and <Eq>{`b = \\lim_{x \\to \\infty}(f(x) - mx)`}</Eq>.

5. **First derivative** <Eq>{`f'`}</Eq>: find critical points. Sign table → intervals of increase (<Eq>{`f' > 0`}</Eq>) and decrease (<Eq>{`f' < 0`}</Eq>), local extrema.

6. **Second derivative** <Eq>{`f''`}</Eq>: find inflection candidates (<Eq>{`f'' = 0`}</Eq> or does not exist). Sign table → concave up (<Eq>{`f'' > 0`}</Eq>) or concave down (<Eq>{`f'' < 0`}</Eq>).

7. **Inflection point:** point <Eq>{`c`}</Eq> where <Eq>{`f''`}</Eq> **changes sign** (concavity swaps).

8. **Sketch:** mark intercepts, asymptotes (dashed lines), extrema and inflections; draw the curve respecting growth and concavity in each interval.

</Definicao>

> "If f'(x) > 0 on an interval, then f is increasing on that interval. If f''(x) > 0, then f is concave up. These two pieces of information, combined with critical and inflection points, give a complete picture of the graph." — [OpenStax Calculus Vol. 1, §4.5](https://openstax.org/books/calculus-volume-1/pages/4-5-derivatives-and-the-shape-of-a-graph)

### Combined behavior table

| <Eq>{`f'`}</Eq> | <Eq>{`f''`}</Eq> | Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| <Eq>{`> 0`}</Eq> | <Eq>{`> 0`}</Eq> | increasing, concave up |
| <Eq>{`> 0`}</Eq> | <Eq>{`< 0`}</Eq> | increasing, concave down |
| <Eq>{`< 0`}</Eq> | <Eq>{`> 0`}</Eq> | decreasing, concave up |
| <Eq>{`< 0`}</Eq> | <Eq>{`< 0`}</Eq> | decreasing, concave down |

### Definition of concavity and inflection

<Definicao titulo="Concavity">

<Eq>{`f`}</Eq> is **concave up** on <Eq>{`I`}</Eq> if <Eq>{`f''(x) > 0`}</Eq> for all <Eq>{`x \\in I`}</Eq> — the curve lies above any tangent line. <Eq>{`f`}</Eq> is **concave down** on <Eq>{`I`}</Eq> if <Eq>{`f''(x) < 0`}</Eq>.

</Definicao>

<Definicao titulo="Inflection point">

<Eq>{`c`}</Eq> is an **inflection point** of <Eq>{`f`}</Eq> if <Eq>{`f`}</Eq> is continuous at <Eq>{`c`}</Eq> and <Eq>{`f''`}</Eq> changes sign at <Eq>{`c`}</Eq>.

</Definicao>

<Insight>

<Eq>{`f''(c) = 0`}</Eq> is necessary but not sufficient for an inflection point. Example: <Eq>{`f(x) = x^4`}</Eq> has <Eq>{`f''(0) = 0`}</Eq>, but concavity does not change at <Eq>{`0`}</Eq> — not an inflection.

</Insight>

<Porta nivel="5" titulo="For a 5-year-old">

Imagine you are drawing a mountainous road. The road sometimes goes up, sometimes goes down.

When it goes up, the derivative is positive. When it goes down, it is negative. At the top of the mountain it pauses for a moment before going down — that is the special point.

Now look at the curve of the road. Sometimes the road curves **upward** (like a bowl) and sometimes **curves downward** (like a dome). The place where the curve switches is an inflection point.

Before drawing anything, it is wise to discover these special places. Then you just connect the dots!

</Porta>

<Porta nivel="10" titulo="For a 10-year-old">

To make a good sketch of <Eq>{`f(x)`}</Eq>, follow this checklist:

1. Where does the function exist? (Domain)
2. Where does it cross the axes? (<Eq>{`f(0)`}</Eq> and where <Eq>{`f(x) = 0`}</Eq>)
3. Does it go to infinity somewhere? (Asymptotes)
4. Where does it **go up** (<Eq>{`f' > 0`}</Eq>) or **go down** (<Eq>{`f' < 0`}</Eq>)?
5. Where does it **curve up** (<Eq>{`f'' > 0`}</Eq>) or **curve down** (<Eq>{`f'' < 0`}</Eq>)?

With these 5 items answered, the drawing practically makes itself!

</Porta>

<Porta nivel="15" titulo="For the high school student">

**The sketching pipeline** has 8 steps. You can remember them with the acronym **DISSECF** (Domain, Intercepts, Symmetry, Sign of <Eq>{`f'`}</Eq>, Extrema, Concavity, Inflections, Figure).

**Critical step — the sign table:** divide the real line at the critical and inflection points. In each interval, determine whether <Eq>{`f' > 0`}</Eq> or <Eq>{`< 0`}</Eq> and whether <Eq>{`f'' > 0`}</Eq> or <Eq>{`< 0`}</Eq>. This completely determines the curve's behavior in each section.

**Oblique asymptote:** occurs when degree of numerator = degree of denominator + 1 in rational functions. Perform polynomial long division to find <Eq>{`y = mx + b`}</Eq>.

**Inflection ≠ critical point:** local extrema have <Eq>{`f' = 0`}</Eq>; inflections have <Eq>{`f'' = 0`}</Eq> (with sign change). A point can be both (rare).

</Porta>

<Porta nivel="25" titulo="For the engineering student">

Sketching via calculus is the **complete qualitative analysis** of a function, combining:

**Mean Value Theorem (MVT):** establishes that <Eq>{`f' > 0`}</Eq> implies strict growth. Without the MVT, knowing only that <Eq>{`f'(c) > 0`}</Eq> at a point does not guarantee growth over an interval.

**Concavity and f'':** if <Eq>{`f''`}</Eq> exists and is positive on <Eq>{`I`}</Eq>, then <Eq>{`f'`}</Eq> is increasing on <Eq>{`I`}</Eq> — the function is "accelerating". In numerical analysis, concavity affects the accuracy of integration methods (the trapezoidal rule overestimates when <Eq>{`f'' < 0`}</Eq>).

**Oblique asymptotes:** <Eq>{`f(x) = P(x)/Q(x)`}</Eq> with <Eq>{`\\deg P = \\deg Q + 1`}</Eq>: perform Euclidean division. The quotient is the oblique asymptote; the remainder <Eq>{`\\to 0`}</Eq> as <Eq>{`x \\to \\infty`}</Eq>.

**Curve in engineering:** signal analysis (Bode diagrams), load-displacement curves in structures, and P-V curves in thermodynamics are direct applications of this qualitative analysis — it substitutes heavy numerical calculation when qualitative behavior suffices.

</Porta>

<Porta nivel="40" titulo="For the senior professional">

The **Kurvendiskussion** (curve discussion) from German curriculum, mandatory in the Abitur, is exactly this pipeline — one of the rare topics where German, Japanese, and Singaporean curricula converge in detail. It represents the operational synthesis of single-variable differential calculus.

Historically, Leibniz and Johann Bernoulli already used the second derivative to classify extrema in the 17th century. Modern formalization — including concavity as a global (not pointwise) property — came with Cauchy and Weierstrass in the 19th century, when analysis replaced intuitive geometric argument with rigorous analytic proof.

In quantitative finance, **convexity** of a bond portfolio is exactly the second derivative of price with respect to interest rate. Risk managers use convexity (positive or negative) to price the curvature of payoffs — directly analogous to analyzing <Eq>{`f''`}</Eq> in this lesson.

In machine learning, loss surface analysis via gradient (<Eq>{`f'`}</Eq>) and Hessian (<Eq>{`f''`}</Eq> in high dimensions) is the direct extension. Saddle points (where gradient vanishes but it is not a minimum) are the optimizer's nightmare — exact analog to <Eq>{`f'(c) = 0`}</Eq> without an extremum, already seen in L61.

</Porta>

<Porta nivel="pratica" titulo="Brazilian practical application">

**Analysis of agricultural production curve.** Soybean yield per hectare, as a function of fertilizer amount applied, has typical behavior:

<Eq display>{`P(x) = -0.01x^3 + 0.6x^2 - 2x + 10, \quad x \in [0, 40]`}</Eq>

where <Eq>{`x`}</Eq> is kg of fertilizer per hectare and <Eq>{`P`}</Eq> is bushels per hectare.

<Eq display>{`P'(x) = -0.03x^2 + 1.2x - 2`}</Eq>

Zeros of <Eq>{`P'`}</Eq>: discriminant <Eq>{`1.44 - 0.24 = 1.20`}</Eq>. Roots: <Eq>{`x \\approx 1.84`}</Eq> and <Eq>{`x \\approx 38.16`}</Eq>. On the interval <Eq>{`[0, 40]`}</Eq>: production grows from <Eq>{`x \\approx 1.84`}</Eq> to <Eq>{`x \\approx 38.16`}</Eq>.

<Eq>{`P''(x) = -0.06x + 1.2 = 0 \\Rightarrow x = 20`}</Eq> — inflection point where the growth rate begins to decline (marginal productivity decreasing, fundamental principle of agricultural microeconomics).

**Practical reading:** applying up to 38 kg/hectare increases production; beyond that, excess fertilizer begins to harm. The inflection point at 20 kg indicates where marginal productivity is maximum.

</Porta>

## Worked examples


<Exemplo titulo="Complete sketch of a cubic polynomial">

**Problem.** Sketch completely <Eq>{`f(x) = x^3 - 3x`}</Eq>.

**Strategy.** Apply the 8 steps of the pipeline.

**Solution.**

1. **Domain:** <Eq>{`\\mathbb{R}`}</Eq>.
2. **Intercepts:** <Eq>{`f(0) = 0`}</Eq>. Roots: <Eq>{`x(x^2-3) = 0 \\Rightarrow x = 0, \\pm\\sqrt{3}`}</Eq>.
3. **Symmetry:** <Eq>{`f(-x) = -x^3 + 3x = -f(x)`}</Eq> → odd.
4. **Asymptotes:** none (polynomial).
5. **<Eq>{`f'(x) = 3x^2 - 3 = 3(x-1)(x+1)`}</Eq>**. Critical points: <Eq>{`x = \\pm 1`}</Eq>. Increasing on <Eq>{`(-\\infty,-1)`}</Eq> and <Eq>{`(1,\\infty)`}</Eq>; decreasing on <Eq>{`(-1,1)`}</Eq>. Local max <Eq>{`f(-1) = 2`}</Eq>; local min <Eq>{`f(1) = -2`}</Eq>.
6. **<Eq>{`f''(x) = 6x`}</Eq>**. Zero at <Eq>{`x = 0`}</Eq>. Concave down for <Eq>{`x < 0`}</Eq>; concave up for <Eq>{`x > 0`}</Eq>.
7. **Inflection:** <Eq>{`x = 0`}</Eq> (sign change of <Eq>{`f''`}</Eq>). Point: <Eq>{`(0, 0)`}</Eq>.
8. **Sketch:** S-shaped curve through the origin, maximum at <Eq>{`(-1, 2)`}</Eq>, minimum at <Eq>{`(1, -2)`}</Eq>.

**Verification.** By odd symmetry, the graph is symmetric about the origin — the values check out.

**Source.** [OpenStax Calculus Vol. 1, §4.5, Example 4.20](https://openstax.org/books/calculus-volume-1/pages/4-5-derivatives-and-the-shape-of-a-graph) · CC-BY-NC-SA

</Exemplo>

<Exemplo titulo="Rational function with horizontal asymptote">

**Problem.** Sketch <Eq>{`f(x) = \\dfrac{x^2}{x^2 + 1}`}</Eq>.

**Strategy.** Identify asymptotes, use <Eq>{`f'`}</Eq> and <Eq>{`f''`}</Eq>.

**Solution.**

1. **Domain:** <Eq>{`\\mathbb{R}`}</Eq> (denominator never zero).
2. **Intercept:** <Eq>{`f(0) = 0`}</Eq>. Only root: <Eq>{`x = 0`}</Eq>.
3. **Symmetry:** <Eq>{`f(-x) = f(x)`}</Eq> → even.
4. **Horizontal asymptote:** <Eq>{`\\lim_{x \\to \\pm\\infty} x^2/(x^2+1) = 1`}</Eq>. Line <Eq>{`y = 1`}</Eq>.
5. **<Eq>{`f'(x) = 2x/(x^2+1)^2`}</Eq>**. Positive for <Eq>{`x > 0`}</Eq>, negative for <Eq>{`x < 0`}</Eq>. Local minimum at <Eq>{`x = 0`}</Eq> (by sign: <Eq>{`f'`}</Eq> changes from <Eq>{`-`}</Eq> to <Eq>{`+`}</Eq>).
6. **<Eq>{`f''(x) = (2(x^2+1)^2 - 2x \\cdot 2(x^2+1)\\cdot 2x)/(x^2+1)^4 = 2(1-3x^2)/(x^2+1)^3`}</Eq>**. Zero: <Eq>{`x = \\pm 1/\\sqrt{3}`}</Eq>.
7. **Inflections at** <Eq>{`x = \\pm 1/\\sqrt{3}`}</Eq>. <Eq>{`f'' > 0`}</Eq> when <Eq>{`|x| < 1/\\sqrt{3}`}</Eq>; <Eq>{`f'' < 0`}</Eq> when <Eq>{`|x| > 1/\\sqrt{3}`}</Eq>.
8. Symmetric curve, starting from 0, increasing asymptotically to 1.

**Verification.** <Eq>{`0 \\leq f(x) < 1`}</Eq> for all <Eq>{`x`}</Eq> — consistent with horizontal asymptote at 1.

**Source.** [APEX Calculus §3.5, Example 3.5.2](https://www.apexcalculus.com/) · CC-BY-NC

</Exemplo>

<Exemplo titulo="Function with vertical and horizontal asymptote">

**Problem.** Sketch <Eq>{`f(x) = \\dfrac{2x}{x - 1}`}</Eq>.

**Strategy.** Identify vertical asymptote (<Eq>{`x = 1`}</Eq>) and horizontal, then analyze <Eq>{`f'`}</Eq>.

**Solution.**

1. **Domain:** <Eq>{`\\mathbb{R} \\setminus \\{1\\}`}</Eq>.
2. **Intercepts:** <Eq>{`f(0) = 0`}</Eq>; root: <Eq>{`x = 0`}</Eq>.
3. **Vertical asymptote:** <Eq>{`x = 1`}</Eq> (<Eq>{`f \\to \\pm\\infty`}</Eq>).
4. **Horizontal asymptote:** <Eq>{`\\lim_{x \\to \\pm\\infty} 2x/(x-1) = 2`}</Eq>. Line <Eq>{`y = 2`}</Eq>.
5. **<Eq>{`f'(x) = (2(x-1) - 2x)/(x-1)^2 = -2/(x-1)^2 < 0`}</Eq>** for all <Eq>{`x \\neq 1`}</Eq>. Function strictly decreasing on each branch.
6. **<Eq>{`f''(x) = 4/(x-1)^3`}</Eq>**. Positive for <Eq>{`x > 1`}</Eq> (concave up); negative for <Eq>{`x < 1`}</Eq> (concave down).
7. **No inflections** (denominator never zero away from <Eq>{`x = 1`}</Eq>).
8. Two branches: <Eq>{`x < 1`}</Eq> (decreasing, concave down) and <Eq>{`x > 1`}</Eq> (decreasing, concave up).

**Verification.** <Eq>{`f`}</Eq> never crosses the asymptote <Eq>{`y = 2`}</Eq> (check: <Eq>{`2x/(x-1) = 2 \\Rightarrow 2x = 2x - 2`}</Eq>, impossible).

**Source.** [Active Calculus §3.2, Activity 3.2.3](https://activecalculus.org/single/sec-3-4-families.html) · CC-BY-NC-SA

</Exemplo>

<Exemplo titulo="Inflection point and concavity">

**Problem.** Determine the inflection points and concavity intervals of <Eq>{`f(x) = x^4 - 6x^2`}</Eq>.

**Strategy.** Compute <Eq>{`f''`}</Eq>, find zeros, verify sign change.

**Solution.**

<Eq display>{`f'(x) = 4x^3 - 12x, \quad f''(x) = 12x^2 - 12 = 12(x-1)(x+1)`}</Eq>

Zeros of <Eq>{`f''`}</Eq>: <Eq>{`x = \\pm 1`}</Eq>.

- <Eq>{`x < -1`}</Eq>: <Eq>{`f'' > 0`}</Eq> → concave up.
- <Eq>{`-1 < x < 1`}</Eq>: <Eq>{`f'' < 0`}</Eq> → concave down.
- <Eq>{`x > 1`}</Eq>: <Eq>{`f'' > 0`}</Eq> → concave up.

Inflections at <Eq>{`x = -1`}</Eq> and <Eq>{`x = 1`}</Eq>: <Eq>{`f(-1) = 1 - 6 = -5`}</Eq>, <Eq>{`f(1) = -5`}</Eq>.

**Verification.** Sign change test of <Eq>{`f''`}</Eq> confirmed. Note: <Eq>{`f(x) = x^4 - 6x^2`}</Eq> has <Eq>{`f''(0) = -12 < 0`}</Eq> and <Eq>{`f''(2) = 36 > 0`}</Eq>, consistent.

**Source.** [OpenStax Calculus Vol. 1, §4.5, Example 4.22](https://openstax.org/books/calculus-volume-1/pages/4-5-derivatives-and-the-shape-of-a-graph) · CC-BY-NC-SA

</Exemplo>

<Exemplo titulo="Rational function with oblique asymptote">

**Problem.** Sketch <Eq>{`f(x) = \\dfrac{x^2 + 1}{x}`}</Eq>.

**Strategy.** Polynomial long division to get oblique asymptote; analyze <Eq>{`f'`}</Eq> and <Eq>{`f''`}</Eq>.

**Solution.**

1. **Domain:** <Eq>{`\\mathbb{R} \\setminus \\{0\\}`}</Eq>.
2. **Intercepts:** <Eq>{`f(x) = 0 \\Rightarrow x^2 + 1 = 0`}</Eq> — no real roots. No intercept at <Eq>{`x = 0`}</Eq> (outside domain).
3. **Symmetry:** <Eq>{`f(-x) = (-x^2 - 1)/(-x) = (x^2+1)/x = f(x)`}</Eq>... wait: <Eq>{`f(-x) = (x^2+1)/(-x) = -f(x)`}</Eq> → odd.
4. **Vertical asymptote:** <Eq>{`x = 0`}</Eq>. Division: <Eq>{`f(x) = x + 1/x`}</Eq> → oblique asymptote <Eq>{`y = x`}</Eq>.
5. **<Eq>{`f'(x) = 1 - 1/x^2`}</Eq>**. Zero: <Eq>{`x = \\pm 1`}</Eq>. Local minimum at <Eq>{`x = 1`}</Eq> (value 2); local maximum at <Eq>{`x = -1`}</Eq> (value −2).
6. **<Eq>{`f''(x) = 2/x^3`}</Eq>**. Positive for <Eq>{`x > 0`}</Eq>, negative for <Eq>{`x < 0`}</Eq>. No inflections (discontinuity at 0).
7. **Sketch:** two hyperbolic branches, each approaching <Eq>{`y = x`}</Eq> and <Eq>{`x = 0`}</Eq>.

**Verification.** <Eq>{`f(x) - x = 1/x \\to 0`}</Eq> as <Eq>{`x \\to \\pm\\infty`}</Eq> — oblique asymptote confirmed.

**Source.** [APEX Calculus §3.5, Example 3.5.4](https://www.apexcalculus.com/) · CC-BY-NC

</Exemplo>
