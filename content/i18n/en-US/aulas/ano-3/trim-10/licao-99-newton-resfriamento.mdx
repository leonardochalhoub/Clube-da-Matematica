<Porta nivel="formal" titulo="Rigorous derivation and solution">

### The law and its hypothesis

The rate of change of an object's temperature is proportional to its deviation from the environment:

<Equation explicacao="k > 0 is the heat transfer constant [1/time]. The negative sign indicates the object cools when T > T_amb and heats when T < T_amb." latex="\frac{dT}{dt} = -k(T - T_{\text{amb}})" />

<Definicao titulo="Solution by separation of variables">

Let $u(t) = T(t) - T_{\text{amb}}$ (temperature excess). Then $u' = T'$ and the equation becomes $u' = -ku$, whose solution is $u = u_0 e^{-kt}$.

Returning to the original temperature:

<Equation explicacao="T_0 = T(0) is the initial temperature; T_amb is the equilibrium temperature. As t -> infinity, T(t) -> T_amb." latex="T(t) = T_{\text{amb}} + (T_0 - T_{\text{amb}})\,e^{-kt}" />

</Definicao>

> "The temperature of a body changes at a rate proportional to the difference between the temperature of the body and the temperature of the surrounding medium. This is *Newton's law of cooling*." — [Trench, Elementary Differential Equations §4.2](https://digitalcommons.trinity.edu/mono/9/)

### Time constant and half-life

<Definicao titulo="Temporal parameters">

- **Time constant** $\tau = 1/k$: at $t = \tau$, the difference $T - T_{\text{amb}}$ drops to $e^{-1} \approx 37\%$ of its initial value.
- **Half-life** $t_{1/2} = \ln 2/k \approx 0.693/k$: at $t_{1/2}$, the difference drops to 50%.
- At $t = 5\tau$: difference drops to $e^{-5} \approx 0.67\%$ — practically at equilibrium.

</Definicao>

### Determining $k$ from data

Given $T(t_1) = T_1$:

<Equation explicacao="Isolates k directly from a measurement at time t_1." latex="k = -\frac{1}{t_1}\ln\!\left(\frac{T_1 - T_{\text{amb}}}{T_0 - T_{\text{amb}}}\right)" />

### Model validity

<Definicao titulo="Conditions for applicability">

The model is valid when:
1. The **temperature difference is small** (linear regime of convective heat transfer).
2. The **ambient temperature is constant** (environment large enough).
3. Heat transfer is by **dominant convection** — not radiation (Stefan-Boltzmann law $\sigma T^4$, nonlinear).

For large differences or high temperatures (industrial furnaces, atmospheric re-entry), use Stefan-Boltzmann or nonlinear convection models.

</Definicao>

<Insight>
Newton's law of cooling is mathematically identical to radioactive decay ($N' = -kN$) and Malthusian growth ($P' = rP$). Every linear autonomous first-order ODE has an exponential solution. The "k" has dimension [1/time] in all these cases—what changes is the physical interpretation.
</Insight>

<Porta nivel="5" titulo="For a 5-year-old">

Have you ever noticed that **hot coffee cools** faster when it's very hot, and very slowly when it's almost at the temperature of the environment?

That's what Newton discovered: the more different the object's temperature is from the environment, the faster it changes. A cup at 90 °C in a 20 °C environment cools much faster than the same cup at 25 °C.

Over time, the coffee always reaches the temperature of the environment—it doesn't cool anymore. It never gets colder than the air around it.

</Porta>

<Porta nivel="10" titulo="For a 10-year-old">

**Newton's law of cooling** says that how fast something cools depends on how different it is from the environment.

Equation: $\dfrac{dT}{dt} = -k(T - T_{\text{amb}})$.

This means: if the temperature difference is 70 °C, the cooling rate is 70 times faster than if the difference is just 1 °C.

The solution is an exponential curve that descends smoothly to equilibrium:

$$T(t) = T_{\text{amb}} + (T_0 - T_{\text{amb}})\,e^{-kt}$$

For example: coffee at 90 °C, environment at 20 °C, $k = 0.05$ min$^{-1}$. In 10 minutes: $T = 20 + 70\,e^{-0.5} \approx 20 + 42.5 = 62.5$ °C.

</Porta>

<Porta nivel="15" titulo="For the high school student">

**Newton's law of cooling** is a separable first-order ODE:

$$\frac{dT}{dt} = -k(T - T_{\text{amb}})$$

**How to solve:**

1. Substitute $u = T - T_{\text{amb}}$: $u' = -ku$.
2. Separate: $\dfrac{du}{u} = -k\,dt$.
3. Integrate: $\ln|u| = -kt + C$.
4. Exponentiate: $u = Ae^{-kt}$.
5. Initial condition $T(0) = T_0$: $A = T_0 - T_{\text{amb}}$.
6. Solution: $T(t) = T_{\text{amb}} + (T_0 - T_{\text{amb}})e^{-kt}$.

**How to determine $k$?** Measure $T$ at two times. For example, if $T(0) = 90$ and $T(30) = 40$ with $T_{\text{amb}} = 20$:

$$k = -\frac{\ln\bigl(20/70\bigr)}{30} = \frac{\ln(70/20)}{30} = \frac{\ln(3.5)}{30} \approx \frac{1.253}{30} \approx 0.0418 \text{ min}^{-1}$$

</Porta>

<Porta nivel="25" titulo="For the engineering student">

The ODE $T' = -k(T - T_{\text{amb}})$ is linear of first order. Written in standard form:

$$T' + kT = kT_{\text{amb}}$$

The integrating factor is $e^{kt}$: multiplying, $(e^{kt}T)' = kT_{\text{amb}}e^{kt}$. Integrating: $T = T_{\text{amb}} + Ce^{-kt}$.

**Energy:** the heat flux $Q' = -hA(T-T_{\text{amb}})$ corresponds to $k = hA/(mc_p)$ where $h$ is the convection coefficient [W/(m²K)], $A$ is surface area, $m$ is mass, and $c_p$ is specific heat.

**Biot number:** $\text{Bi} = hL/\kappa$, where $L$ is thickness and $\kappa$ is conductivity. Newton's law of cooling (thermally thin body) is valid for $\text{Bi} \ll 1$—internal conduction resistance is negligible compared to surface convection.

**Generalization:** if $T_{\text{amb}}(t)$ varies with time, the ODE is still linear first order and solves with an integrating factor. If internal heat generation is constant ($Q_{\text{int}}$), the equilibrium solution changes: $T_{\infty} = T_{\text{amb}} + Q_{\text{int}}/(hA)$.

**Inverse application (forensic):** given $T_{\text{mort}} = 37$ °C, $T_{\text{amb}}$ and corpse temperature measured at two times after death, we determine $k$ and then time of death. Standard method in forensic medicine.

</Porta>

<Porta nivel="40" titulo="For the senior professional">

Newton's law of cooling, published in 1701 in the *Philosophical Transactions* (Scala graduum caloris), is one of the first quantitative mathematical models of a physical phenomenon. Newton measured the temperature of heated metals by comparing them to the cooling rate of reference bodies—pioneering the concept of calibration.

**Thermodynamic perspective:** the law is the first-order linearization of heat exchange by natural convection. For radiation transfer, the exact model is $Q \propto T^4 - T_{\text{amb}}^4$ (Stefan-Boltzmann law), which linearizes to $Q \approx 4\sigma T_{\text{amb}}^3 (T - T_{\text{amb}})$ for small differences—recovering Newton with $k_{\text{rad}} = 4\sigma T_{\text{amb}}^3 A/(mc_p)$.

**Advanced forensic body temperature models:** the Henssge model (1979) uses a nonlinear differential equation incorporating the effect of body insulation and spatial gradients. Accepted by German courts as standard (Richtlinien der Deutschen Gesellschaft für Gerichtliche Medizin).

**Temperature control in industrial engineering:** pasteurizers, cold chambers and tubular reactors in the food industry (Nestlé, BRF, JBS in Brazil) use the Newtonian model with overall transfer coefficient $U$ to size heat exchangers via LMTD (Log Mean Temperature Difference)—extension of Newton's law to continuous flow.

</Porta>

<Porta nivel="pratica" titulo="Applications with Brazilian data">

**Forensic medicine — estimation of time of death.** Body found at 2 PM with corpse temperature of 30 °C. Ambient temperature: 22 °C. $k = 0.08$ h$^{-1}$ (average adult body). Normal body temperature: 37 °C. When did death occur?

$30 = 22 + 15\,e^{-0.08\,t} \Rightarrow e^{-0.08\,t} = 8/15 \Rightarrow t = -\ln(8/15)/0.08 \approx 0.629/0.08 \approx 7.9$ h.

Death occurred approximately 7.9 hours ago—around 6 AM.

**Milk pasteurization (UHT).** Brazil's Ministry of Agriculture requires pasteurization at 72 °C for 15 s. After heating, milk must cool from 72 °C to 4 °C before packaging. With $T_{\text{amb}}^{\text{cold}} = 0$ °C and $k = 0.15$ s$^{-1}$ in the industrial heat exchanger:

$t = -\ln(4/72)/0.15 = \ln(18)/0.15 \approx 2.89/0.15 \approx 19.3$ s.

The heat exchanger must ensure at least 20 s of contact with the cold surface.

**Cooling data centers in Brazil.** AWS São Paulo servers dissipate heat at 1–5 kW per rack. With $hA = 200$ W/K and $T_{\text{amb}} = 24$ °C: equilibrium temperature $T_{\infty} = 24 + P/(hA) = 24 + 2000/200 = 34$ °C. Above the recommended limit of 27 °C (ASHRAE A1), requiring active cooling.

</Porta>

## Worked examples

<Exemplo numero="99.1" titulo="Direct solution with k given">

**Problem.** Coffee at 90 °C in a 20 °C environment, $k = 0.05$ min$^{-1}$. What is the temperature after 20 minutes? When does it reach 50 °C?

**Strategy.** Apply the formula directly for the first part; solve the exponential equation for the second.

**Solution.**

$T(t) = 20 + 70\,e^{-0.05\,t}$.

$T(20) = 20 + 70\,e^{-1} = 20 + 70 \times 0.3679 \approx 20 + 25.7 = 45.7$ °C.

For $T = 50$: $50 = 20 + 70\,e^{-0.05\,t} \Rightarrow e^{-0.05\,t} = 30/70 \Rightarrow t = -\ln(30/70)/0.05 = \ln(7/3)/0.05 \approx 0.847/0.05 \approx 16.9$ min.

**Verification.** $T(16.9) = 20 + 70\,e^{-0.05 \times 16.9} = 20 + 70\,e^{-0.847} \approx 20 + 30 = 50$ °C ✓.

**Source.** [Lebl, Notes on Diffy Qs §1.6, Example 1.6.2](https://www.jirka.org/diffyqs/html/auteq_section.html) — CC-BY-SA

</Exemplo>

<Exemplo numero="99.2" titulo="Determination of k from two measurements">

**Problem.** Metal bar: $T(0) = 100$ °C, $T_{\text{amb}} = 25$ °C. After 10 min, $T = 60$ °C. Find $k$ and estimate $T(25)$.

**Strategy.** Use $T(t_1) = T_1$ to isolate $k$; substitute into the model.

**Solution.**

$60 = 25 + 75\,e^{-10k} \Rightarrow e^{-10k} = 35/75 = 7/15$.

$k = -\ln(7/15)/10 = \ln(15/7)/10 \approx 0.7608/10 \approx 0.07608$ min$^{-1}$.

$T(25) = 25 + 75\,e^{-0.07608 \times 25} = 25 + 75\,e^{-1.902} \approx 25 + 75 \times 0.1493 \approx 36.2$ °C.

**Verification.** $T(10) = 25 + 75 \times (7/15) = 25 + 35 = 60$ °C ✓.

**Source.** [OpenStax Calculus Vol. 2, §4.4, Example 4.4](https://openstax.org/books/calculus-volume-2/pages/4-4-the-logistic-equation) — CC-BY-NC-SA

</Exemplo>

<Exemplo numero="99.3" titulo="Forensic estimate of time of death">

**Problem.** Body found at 10 PM with $T = 33$ °C. Constant ambient temperature: 18 °C. Normal body temperature: 37 °C. $k = 0.06$ h$^{-1}$. When did death occur?

**Strategy.** Take the moment of death as $t=0$ and solve for $t$.

**Solution.**

$T(t) = 18 + 19\,e^{-0.06\,t}$.

$33 = 18 + 19\,e^{-0.06\,t} \Rightarrow e^{-0.06\,t} = 15/19$.

$t = -\ln(15/19)/0.06 = \ln(19/15)/0.06 \approx 0.2364/0.06 \approx 3.94$ h before 10 PM.

Death occurred around 6 PM (approximately 6:03 PM).

**Verification.** $T(3.94) = 18 + 19\,e^{-0.06 \times 3.94} \approx 18 + 19 \times 0.789 \approx 18 + 15 = 33$ °C ✓.

**Source.** [OpenStax Calculus Vol. 2, §4.4](https://openstax.org/books/calculus-volume-2/pages/4-4-the-logistic-equation) — CC-BY-NC-SA

</Exemplo>

<Exemplo numero="99.4" titulo="Equilibrium with time-varying ambient temperature (Euler model)">

**Problem.** $T_{\text{amb}}(t) = 20 + 5\sin(2\pi t/24)$ °C (daily variation). $T(0) = 15$ °C, $k = 0.1$ h$^{-1}$. Set up the ODE and use 3 Euler steps with $h = 1$ h.

**Strategy.** The ODE is $T' = -k(T - T_{\text{amb}}(t))$—non-autonomous but still linear. Apply Euler.

**Solution.**

$T_{\text{amb}}(0) = 20 + 0 = 20$. $f(0, 15) = -0.1(15-20) = 0.5$.

$T_1 = 15 + 1 \times 0.5 = 15.5$ °C.

$T_{\text{amb}}(1) = 20 + 5\sin(15°) \approx 20 + 1.294 = 21.294$. $f(1, 15.5) = -0.1(15.5 - 21.294) = 0.5794$.

$T_2 = 15.5 + 0.5794 = 16.079$ °C.

$T_{\text{amb}}(2) = 20 + 5\sin(30°) = 22.5$. $f(2, 16.079) = -0.1(16.079 - 22.5) = 0.6421$.

$T_3 = 16.079 + 0.6421 \approx 16.721$ °C.

**Verification.** The temperature increases because $T < T_{\text{amb}}$ in all three steps ✓.

**Source.** [Lebl, Notes on Diffy Qs §1.6](https://www.jirka.org/diffyqs/html/auteq_section.html) — CC-BY-SA

</Exemplo>

<Exemplo numero="99.5" titulo="Time constant and residual percentage">

**Problem.** Industrial refrigerator: $T_0 = 80$ °C (freshly cooked product), $T_{\text{amb}} = -10$ °C (cold chamber), $k = 0.03$ min$^{-1}$. Calculate the time constant $\tau$ and verify that at $t = 5\tau$ the product is within 1% of the final temperature.

**Strategy.** Use $\tau = 1/k$; calculate $T(5\tau)$ and verify.

**Solution.**

$\tau = 1/0.03 \approx 33.3$ min.

Initial difference: $T_0 - T_{\text{amb}} = 80 - (-10) = 90$ °C.

$T(5\tau) = -10 + 90\,e^{-5} = -10 + 90 \times 0.00674 \approx -10 + 0.607 = -9.39$ °C.

Residual difference: $-9.39 - (-10) = 0.61$ °C, which is $0.61/90 \approx 0.67\%$ of the initial difference.

At $5\tau \approx 167$ min $\approx 2.8$ hours, the product had reached practical equilibrium.

**Verification.** $e^{-5} = 0.00674 < 0.01$ ✓ — within 1%.

**Source.** [Trench, Elementary Differential Equations §4.2](https://digitalcommons.trinity.edu/mono/9/) — open access

</Exemplo>
