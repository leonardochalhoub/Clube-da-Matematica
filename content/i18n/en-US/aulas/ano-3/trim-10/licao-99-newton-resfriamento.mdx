<Porta nivel="formal" titulo="Rigorous derivation and solution">

### The law and its hypothesis

The rate of change of an object's temperature is proportional to the deviation from the environment:

<Equation explicacao="k > 0 is the heat transfer constant [1/time]. The negative sign indicates that the object cools when T > T_amb and warms when T < T_amb." latex="\frac{dT}{dt} = -k(T - T_{\text{amb}})" />

<Definicao titulo="Solution by separation of variables">

Let $u(t) = T(t) - T_{\text{amb}}$ (temperature excess). Then $u' = T'$ and the equation becomes $u' = -ku$, whose solution is $u = u_0 e^{-kt}$.

Returning to the original temperature:

<Equation explicacao="T_0 = T(0) is the initial temperature; T_amb is the equilibrium temperature. For t -> inf, T(t) -> T_amb." latex="T(t) = T_{\text{amb}} + (T_0 - T_{\text{amb}})\,e^{-kt}" />

</Definicao>

> "The temperature of a body changes at a rate proportional to the difference between the temperature of the body and the temperature of the surrounding medium. This is *Newton's law of cooling*." — [Trench, Elementary Differential Equations §4.2](https://digitalcommons.trinity.edu/mono/9/)

### Time constant and half-life

<Definicao titulo="Temporal parameters">

- **Time constant** $\tau = 1/k$: at $t = \tau$, the difference $T - T_{\text{amb}}$ drops to $e^{-1} \approx 37\%$ of the initial value.
- **Half-life** $t_{1/2} = \ln 2/k \approx 0{,}693/k$: at $t_{1/2}$, the difference drops to 50%.
- At $t = 5\tau$: the difference drops to $e^{-5} \approx 0{,}67\%$ — practically in equilibrium.

</Definicao>

### Determining $k$ from data

Given $T(t_1) = T_1$:

<Equation explicacao="k is isolated directly from a measurement at time t_1." latex="k = -\frac{1}{t_1}\ln\!\left(\frac{T_1 - T_{\text{amb}}}{T_0 - T_{\text{amb}}}\right)" />

### Model validity

<Definicao titulo="Applicability conditions">

The model is valid when:
1. The **temperature difference is small** (linear regime of heat transfer by convection).
2. The **ambient temperature is constant** (environment large enough).
3. Heat transfer is **convection-dominated** — not radiation (Stefan-Boltzmann law $\sigma T^4$, non-linear).

For large differences or high temperatures (industrial furnaces, atmospheric reentry), the Stefan-Boltzmann model or non-linear convection models are used.

</Definicao>

<Insight>
Newton's law of cooling is mathematically identical to radioactive decay ($N' = -kN$) and Malthusian growth ($P' = rP$). Every 1st-order linear autonomous ODE has an exponential solution. The "k" has dimensions of [1/time] in all these cases — what changes is the physical interpretation.
</Insight>

<Porta nivel="5" titulo="For a 5-year-old">

Have you ever noticed that <strong>hot coffee cools down</strong> faster when it's very hot, and very slowly when it's almost at room temperature?

That's what Newton discovered: the more different the object's temperature is from the environment, the faster it changes. A cup at 90 °C in a 20 °C room cools down much faster than the same cup at 25 °C.

Over time, the coffee always reaches the room temperature — it doesn't get any cooler. It never gets colder than the air around it.

</Porta>

<Porta nivel="10" titulo="For a 10-year-old">

<strong>Newton's law of cooling</strong> says that the speed at which something cools depends on how different it is from the environment.

Equation: $\dfrac{dT}{dt} = -k(T - T_{\text{amb}})$.

This means: if the temperature difference is 70 °C, the cooling rate is 70 times higher than if the difference is only 1 °C.

The solution is an exponential curve that descends smoothly to equilibrium:

$$T(t) = T_{\text{amb}} + (T_0 - T_{\text{amb}})\,e^{-kt}$$

For example: coffee at 90 °C, environment at 20 °C, $k = 0{,}05$ min$^{-1}$. In 10 minutes: $T = 20 + 70\,e^{-0{,}5} \approx 20 + 42{,}5 = 62{,}5$ °C.

</Porta>

<Porta nivel="15" titulo="For the high school student">

<strong>Newton's law of cooling</strong> is a 1st-order separable ODE:

$$\frac{dT}{dt} = -k(T - T_{\text{amb}})$$

<strong>How to solve:</strong>

1. Substitute $u = T - T_{\text{amb}}$: $u' = -ku$.
2. Separate: $\dfrac{du}{u} = -k\,dt$.
3. Integrate: $\ln|u| = -kt + C$.
4. Exponentiate: $u = Ae^{-kt}$.
5. Initial condition $T(0) = T_0$: $A = T_0 - T_{\text{amb}}$.
6. Solution: $T(t) = T_{\text{amb}} + (T_0 - T_{\text{amb}})e^{-kt}$.

<strong>How to determine $k$?</strong> Measure $T$ at two instants. For example, if $T(0) = 90$ and $T(30) = 40$ with $T_{\text{amb}} = 20$:

$$k = -\frac{\ln\bigl(20/70\bigr)}{30} = \frac{\ln(70/20)}{30} = \frac{\ln(3{,}5)}{30} \approx \frac{1{,}253}{30} \approx 0{,}0418 \text{ min}^{-1}$$

</Porta>

<Porta nivel="25" titulo="For the engineering student">

The ODE $T' = -k(T - T_{\text{amb}})$ is a 1st-order linear ODE. Written in standard form:

$$T' + kT = kT_{\text{amb}}$$

The integrating factor is $e^{kt}$: multiplying, $(e^{kt}T)' = kT_{\text{amb}}e^{kt}$. Integrating: $T = T_{\text{amb}} + Ce^{-kt}$.

<strong>Energy:</strong> the heat flux $Q' = -hA(T-T_{\text{amb}})$ corresponds to $k = hA/(mc_p)$ where $h$ is the convection coefficient [W/(m²K)], $A$ is the surface area, $m$ is the mass, and $c_p$ is the specific heat.

<strong>Biot number:</strong> $\text{Bi} = hL/\kappa$, where $L$ is the thickness and $\kappa$ is the conductivity. Newton's law of cooling ("thermally thin" body) is valid for $\text{Bi} \ll 1$ — internal conduction resistance is negligible compared to surface convection.

<strong>Generalization:</strong> if $T_{\text{amb}}(t)$ varies with time, the ODE is still 1st-order linear and is solved with an integrating factor. If the internal heat source is constant ($Q_{\text{int}}$), the equilibrium solution changes: $T_{\infty} = T_{\text{amb}} + Q_{\text{int}}/(hA)$.

<strong>Inverse application (forensics):</strong> given $T_{\text{body}} = 37$ °C, $T_{\text{amb}}$, and the body temperature measured at two instants after death, $k$ is determined and then the time of death. Standard method in forensic medicine.

</Porta>

<Porta nivel="40" titulo="For the senior professional">

Newton's law of cooling, published in 1701 in <em>Philosophical Transactions</em> (Scala graduum caloris), is one of the first quantitative mathematical models of a physical phenomenon. Newton measured the temperature of heated metals by comparing them with the cooling rate of reference bodies — a pioneer in the concept of calibration.

<strong>Thermodynamic perspective:</strong> the law is the first-order linearization of heat exchange by natural convection. For radiation transfer, the exact model is $Q \propto T^4 - T_{\text{amb}}^4$ (Stefan-Boltzmann Law), which linearizes to $Q \approx 4\sigma T_{\text{amb}}^3 (T - T_{\text{amb}})$ for small differences — recovering Newton with $k_{\text{rad}} = 4\sigma T_{\text{amb}}^3 A/(mc_p)$.

<strong>Advanced forensic body temperature models:</strong> the Henssge model (1979) uses a non-linear differential equation that incorporates the effect of body insulation and spatial gradients. Accepted by German courts as a standard (Richtlinien der Deutschen Gesellschaft für Gerichtliche Medizin).

<strong>Temperature control in industrial engineering:</strong> pasteurizers, cold storage, and tubular reactors in the food industry use the Newtonian model with a global transfer coefficient $U$ to size heat exchangers via LMTD (Log Mean Temperature Difference) — an extension of Newton's law for continuous flow.

</Porta>

<Porta nivel="pratica" titulo="Applications with data">

<strong>Forensic medicine — estimating time of death.</strong> Body found at 2:00 PM with a body temperature of 30 °C. Ambient temperature: 22 °C. $k = 0{,}08$ h$^{-1}$ (average adult body). Normal temperature: 37 °C. When did death occur?

$30 = 22 + 15\,e^{-0{,}08\,t} \Rightarrow e^{-0{,}08\,t} = 8/15 \Rightarrow t = -\ln(8/15)/0{,}08 \approx 0{,}629/0{,}08 \approx 7{,}9$ h.

Death occurred approximately 7.9 hours ago — around 6:00 AM.

<strong>Milk pasteurization (UHT).</strong> Regulations require pasteurization at 72 °C for 15 s. After heating, the milk must cool from 72 °C to 4 °C before bottling. With $T_{\text{amb}}^{\text{cold}} = 0$ °C and $k = 0{,}15$ s$^{-1}$ in the industrial heat exchanger:

$t = -\ln(4/72)/0{,}15 = \ln(18)/0{,}15 \approx 2{,}89/0{,}15 \approx 19{,}3$ s.

The exchanger must ensure at least 20 s of contact with the cold surface.

<strong>Data center cooling.</strong> Servers dissipate heat at 1–5 kW per rack. With $hA = 200$ W/K and $T_{\text{amb}} = 24$ °C: equilibrium temperature $T_{\infty} = 24 + P/(hA) = 24 + 2000/200 = 34$ °C. Above the recommended limit of 27 °C (ASHRAE A1), requiring active cooling.

</Porta>

## Solved examples

<Exemplo numero="99.1" titulo="Direct solution with given k">

<strong>Problem.</strong> Coffee at 90 °C in a 20 °C environment, $k = 0{,}05$ min$^{-1}$. What is the temperature after 20 minutes? How long until it reaches 50 °C?

<strong>Strategy.</strong> Apply the formula directly for the first item; solve the exponential equation for the second.

<strong>Resolution.</strong>

$T(t) = 20 + 70\,e^{-0{,}05\,t}$.

$T(20) = 20 + 70\,e^{-1} = 20 + 70 \times 0{,}3679 \approx 20 + 25{,}7 = 45{,}7$ °C.

For $T = 50$: $50 = 20 + 70\,e^{-0{,}05\,t} \Rightarrow e^{-0{,}05\,t} = 30/70 \Rightarrow t = -\ln(30/70)/0{,}05 = \ln(7/3)/0{,}05 \approx 0{,}847/0{,}05 \approx 16{,}9$ min.

<strong>Verification.</strong> $T(16{,}9) = 20 + 70\,e^{-0{,}05 \times 16{,}9} = 20 + 70\,e^{-0{,}847} \approx 20 + 30 = 50$ °C ✓.

<strong>Source.</strong> [Lebl, Notes on Diffy Qs §1.6, Example 1.6.2](https://www.jirka.org/diffyqs/html/auteq_section.html) — CC-BY-SA

</Exemplo>

<Exemplo numero="99.2" titulo="Determining k from two measurements">

<strong>Problem.</strong> Metal bar: $T(0) = 100$ °C, $T_{\text{amb}} = 25$ °C. After 10 min, $T = 60$ °C. Find $k$ and estimate $T(25)$.

<strong>Strategy.</strong> Use $T(t_1) = T_1$ to isolate $k$; substitute into the model.

<strong>Resolution.</strong>

$60 = 25 + 75\,e^{-10k} \Rightarrow e^{-10k} = 35/75 = 7/15$.

$k = -\ln(7/15)/10 = \ln(15/7)/10 \approx 0{,}7608/10 \approx 0{,}07608$ min$^{-1}$.

$T(25) = 25 + 75\,e^{-0{,}07608 \times 25} = 25 + 75\,e^{-1{,}902} \approx 25 + 75 \times 0{,}1493 \approx 36{,}2$ °C.

<strong>Verification.</strong> $T(10) = 25 + 75 \times (7/15) = 25 + 35 = 60$ °C ✓.

<strong>Source.</strong> [OpenStax Calculus Vol. 2, §4.4, Example 4.4](https://openstax.org/books/calculus-volume-2/pages/4-4-the-logistic-equation) — CC-BY-NC-SA

</Exemplo>

<Exemplo numero="99.3" titulo="Forensic estimate of time of death">

<strong>Problem.</strong> Body found at 10:00 PM with $T = 33$ °C. Constant ambient temperature: 18 °C. Normal body temperature: 37 °C. $k = 0{,}06$ h$^{-1}$. When did death occur?

<strong>Strategy.</strong> Take the moment of death as $t=0$ and solve for $t$.

<strong>Resolution.</strong>

$T(t) = 18 + 19\,e^{-0{,}06\,t}$.

$33 = 18 + 19\,e^{-0{,}06\,t} \Rightarrow e^{-0{,}06\,t} = 15/19$.

$t = -\ln(15/19)/0{,}06 = \ln(19/15)/0{,}06 \approx 0{,}2364/0{,}06 \approx 3{,}94$ h before 10:00 PM.

Death occurred around 6:00 PM (approximately 6:03 PM).

<strong>Verification.</strong> $T(3{,}94) = 18 + 19\,e^{-0{,}06 \times 3{,}94} \approx 18 + 19 \times 0{,}789 \approx 18 + 15 = 33$ °C ✓.

<strong>Source.</strong> [OpenStax Calculus Vol. 2, §4.4](https://openstax.org/books/calculus-volume-2/pages/4-4-the-logistic-equation) — CC-BY-NC-SA

</Exemplo>

<Exemplo numero="99.4" titulo="Equilibrium with variable ambient temperature (Euler model)">

<strong>Problem.</strong> $T_{\text{amb}}(t) = 20 + 5\sin(2\pi t/24)$ °C (daily variation). $T(0) = 15$ °C, $k = 0{,}1$ h$^{-1}$. Set up the ODE and use 3 Euler steps with $h = 1$ h.

<strong>Strategy.</strong> The ODE is $T' = -k(T - T_{\text{amb}}(t))$ — non-autonomous but still linear. Apply Euler.

<strong>Resolution.</strong>

$T_{\text{amb}}(0) = 20 + 0 = 20$. $f(0, 15) = -0{,}1(15-20) = 0{,}5$.

$T_1 = 15 + 1 \times 0{,}5 = 15{,}5$ °C.

$T_{\text{amb}}(1) = 20 + 5\sin(15°) \approx 20 + 1{,}294 = 21{,}294$. $f(1, 15{,}5) = -0{,}1(15{,}5 - 21{,}294) = 0{,}5794$.

$T_2 = 15{,}5 + 0{,}5794 = 16{,}079$ °C.

$T_{\text{amb}}(2) = 20 + 5\sin(30°) = 22{,}5$. $f(2, 16{,}079) = -0{,}1(16{,}079 - 22{,}5) = 0{,}6421$.

$T_3 = 16{,}079 + 0{,}6421 \approx 16{,}721$ °C.

<strong>Verification.</strong> The temperature rises because $T < T_{\text{amb}}$ in all three steps ✓.

<strong>Source.</strong> [Lebl, Notes on Diffy Qs §1.6](https://www.jirka.org/diffyqs/html/auteq_section.html) — CC-BY-SA

</Exemplo>

<Exemplo numero="99.5" titulo="Time constant and residual percentage">

<strong>Problem.</strong> Industrial refrigerator: $T_0 = 80$ °C (freshly cooked product), $T_{\text{amb}} = -10$ °C (cold chamber), $k = 0{,}03$ min$^{-1}$. Calculate the time constant $\tau$ and verify that at $t = 5\tau$ the product is within 1% of the final temperature.

<strong>Strategy.</strong> Use $\tau = 1/k$; calculate $T(5\tau)$ and verify.

<strong>Resolution.</strong>

$\tau = 1/0{,}03 \approx 33{,}3$ min.

Initial difference: $T_0 - T_{\text{amb}} = 80 - (-10) = 90$ °C.

$T(5\tau) = -10 + 90\,e^{-5} = -10 + 90 \times 0{,}00674 \approx -10 + 0{,}607 = -9{,}39$ °C.

Residual difference: $-9{,}39 - (-10) = 0{,}61$ °C, which is $0{,}61/90 \approx 0{,}67\%$ of the initial difference.

At $5\tau \approx 167$ min $\approx 2{,}8$ hours, the product has practically reached equilibrium.

<strong>Verification.</strong> $e^{-5} = 0{,}00674 < 0{,}01$ ✓ — within 1%.

<strong>Source.</strong> [Trench, Elementary Differential Equations §4.2](https://digitalcommons.trinity.edu/mono/9/) — open

</Exemplo>
