### Equation of motion

<Definicao titulo="Mass-spring-damper model">

$$m\ddot x + c\dot x + kx = F(t)$$

- $m > 0$: mass [kg].
- $c \geq 0$: viscous damping coefficient [N.s/m].
- $k > 0$: spring stiffness constant [N/m].
- $F(t)$: external force [N].

**Dimensionless form:** divide by $m$ and define $\omega_0 = \sqrt{k/m}$, $\zeta = c/(2\sqrt{mk})$:

$$\ddot x + 2\zeta\omega_0\dot x + \omega_0^2 x = F(t)/m$$

</Definicao>

### Characteristic equation and regimes

$\lambda^2 + 2\zeta\omega_0\lambda + \omega_0^2 = 0$. Discriminant $\Delta = 4\omega_0^2(\zeta^2 - 1)$.

<Teorema titulo="Four oscillator regimes">

**Undamped** ($c = 0$, $\zeta = 0$): $\lambda = \pm i\omega_0$.
$$x(t) = A\cos(\omega_0 t) + B\sin(\omega_0 t)$$

**Underdamped** ($0 < \zeta < 1$): $\lambda = -\zeta\omega_0 \pm i\omega_d$, with $\omega_d = \omega_0\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}$.
$$x(t) = e^{-\zeta\omega_0 t}(A\cos\omega_d t + B\sin\omega_d t)$$

**Critically damped** ($\zeta = 1$): double root $\lambda = -\omega_0$.
$$x(t) = (A + Bt)e^{-\omega_0 t}$$

**Overdamped** ($\zeta > 1$): $\lambda_{1,2} = \omega_0(-\zeta \pm \sqrt{\zeta^2-1}) < 0$.
$$x(t) = Ae^{\lambda_1 t} + Be^{\lambda_2 t}$$

</Teorema>

> "The most important case is $b^2 - 4km < 0$, which occurs when the damping is small... In this case the solution oscillates with exponentially decaying amplitude." — [Lebl, Notes on Diffy Qs, §2.4](https://www.jirka.org/diffyqs/html/mech_section.html)

### Harmonic forced response

For $F(t) = F_0\cos\omega t$: particular solution (steady-state)

$$x_p(t) = \frac{F_0/m}{\sqrt{(\omega_0^2-\omega^2)^2 + 4\zeta^2\omega_0^2\omega^2}}\cos(\omega t - \phi)$$

where $\tan\phi = 2\zeta\omega_0\omega/(\omega_0^2 - \omega^2)$.

### Resonance

<Insight>

At $\zeta = 0$: if $\omega = \omega_0$, the particular solution grows without bound — $x_p = \tfrac{F_0}{2m\omega_0} t\sin(\omega_0 t)$. **Pure resonance.** For $\zeta > 0$: the amplitude has a finite maximum at $\omega_r = \omega_0\sqrt{1-2\zeta^2}$ (below $\omega_0$). Amplification factor at $\omega_r$: $Q = 1/(2\zeta)$ — the **quality factor**.

</Insight>

<Porta nivel="5" titulo="For a 5-year-old">

Imagine a spring with a weight hanging from it. You pull the weight and let go. What happens?

- **No damping**: the weight bounces up and down forever, like a perfect swing.
- **Little damping** (underdamped): it bounces several times, but each time a little less, until it stops.
- **Exact damping** (critical): it goes straight to the resting point without bouncing at all. This is what a car's suspension does!
- **Lots of damping** (overdamped): it moves down slowly, like moving through honey.

</Porta>

<Porta nivel="10" titulo="For a 10-year-old">

The equation for a spring with a weight and a damper is exactly the 2nd-order ODE we studied, with physical names:

$$m \ddot x + c \dot x + k x = F(t)$$

$m$ = mass, $k$ = spring stiffness, $c$ = damping (friction), $F(t)$ = force you apply.

The characteristic equation gives $\lambda^2 + (c/m)\lambda + k/m = 0$. What happens depends on $c$:

- $c = 0$: oscillates forever (ideal swing).
- $c$ small: oscillates and dies out.
- $c$ at the exact value: stops without oscillating, as fast as possible.
- $c$ large: stops slowly without oscillating.

Car suspension is designed to be critically damped: it returns to rest quickly, without bouncing.

</Porta>

<Porta nivel="15" titulo="For a 15-year-old">

### Two important parameters

$\omega_0 = \sqrt{k/m}$ — natural frequency [rad/s]. $f_0 = \omega_0/(2\pi)$ — frequency in Hz.

$\zeta = c/(2\sqrt{mk})$ — damping ratio. Dimensionless.

### Algorithm to solve the free oscillator

1. Write $\lambda^2 + 2\zeta\omega_0\lambda + \omega_0^2 = 0$.
2. Calculate $\Delta = 4\omega_0^2(\zeta^2 - 1)$.
3. Apply the corresponding case (see table).

### Forced response and resonance

If $F(t) = F_0\cos\omega t$ and $\zeta = 0$:

- $\omega \neq \omega_0$: oscillation with beats.
- $\omega = \omega_0$: resonance — amplitude grows linearly with $t$.

**Historical example**: the Tacoma Narrows Bridge (1940) entered resonance with the wind and collapsed after 4 hours of increasing oscillation. Vortex frequency = bridge natural frequency.

### Calculating ICs

With $x(0) = x_0$, $\dot x(0) = v_0$: substituting into the general solution, we get a $2 \times 2$ system for $A$ and $B$.

</Porta>

<Porta nivel="25" titulo="For an engineering student">

### Frequency response

For $\ddot x + 2\zeta\omega_0\dot x + \omega_0^2 x = (F_0/m)\cos\omega t$, the steady-state amplitude is:

$$|X(\omega)| = \frac{F_0/k}{\sqrt{(1-r^2)^2 + (2\zeta r)^2}}, \quad r = \frac{\omega}{\omega_0}$$

Resonance peak at $r = \sqrt{1 - 2\zeta^2}$ (for $\zeta < 1/\sqrt{2} \approx 0.707$). Quality factor $Q = 1/(2\zeta)$.

### -3 dB bandwidth

Bandwidth: $\Delta\omega = \zeta\omega_0 \cdot 2$ (in rad/s). For small $\zeta$: $Q = \omega_0/\Delta\omega$.

### Transfer function

$H(s) = (1/m)/(s^2 + 2\zeta\omega_0 s + \omega_0^2)$. Poles: $s = \omega_0(-\zeta \pm \sqrt{\zeta^2-1})$.

For $\zeta < 1$: complex conjugate poles with $|s| = \omega_0$ and angle $\arccos\zeta$.

### Vibration isolation

If $x$ is chassis vibration and $y$ is passenger movement:

$$\ddot y + 2\zeta\omega_0\dot y + \omega_0^2 y = 2\zeta\omega_0\dot x + \omega_0^2 x$$

Transmissibility $|T(\omega)| = \sqrt{1 + (2\zeta r)^2}/\sqrt{(1-r^2)^2 + (2\zeta r)^2}$.

For $r > \sqrt{2}$: $|T| < 1$ — effective isolation. Suspensions are designed with low $\omega_0$ to ensure $r > \sqrt{2}$ for most of the road vibration spectrum.

### Dynamic absorption (neutralizer)

Adding mass $m_2$ with spring $k_2$ to the main system eliminates resonance at $\omega_0$ if $k_2/m_2 = \omega_0^2$. Principle of vibration absorbers in skyscrapers (e.g., Taipei 101).

</Porta>

<Porta nivel="40" titulo="For a senior professional">

### Modal analysis

For systems with $n$ degrees of freedom: $\mathbf{M}\ddot{\mathbf{x}} + \mathbf{C}\dot{\mathbf{x}} + \mathbf{K}\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{F}(t)$. Normal modes are eigenvectors of the problem $(\mathbf{K} - \omega^2\mathbf{M})\boldsymbol{\phi} = \mathbf{0}$. For proportional damping ($\mathbf{C} = \alpha\mathbf{M} + \beta\mathbf{K}$), each mode decouples — modal analysis.

### Green's function response

$G(t,\tau) = \frac{1}{m\omega_d}e^{-\zeta\omega_0(t-\tau)}\sin\omega_d(t-\tau)\,H(t-\tau)$ is the **impulse-response function**. The solution with arbitrary forcing is:

$$x(t) = \int_0^t G(t,\tau) F(\tau)\,d\tau + x_h(t) \quad\text{(Duhamel's integral)}$$

### Gravitational wave detection

The LIGO interferometer is modeled as a 2nd-order oscillator: suspended mirrors with $\omega_0 \approx 1$ Hz, detection frequency 10–2000 Hz. Signal analysis requires the equation $m\ddot x + kx = h(t) L m \omega_L^2$ where $h$ is the metric strain. Kip Thorne, Barry Barish, and Rainer Weiss received the [2017 Nobel Prize in Physics](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/physics/2017/summary/) for this detection — the oscillator model we study here is part of the core analysis.

### Linear model critique

Coulomb friction (solid) is $-\mu mg\,\text{sgn}(\dot x)$ — nonlinear. Structural damping: $c = k\eta/\omega$ (frequency dependent). Nonlinear models: Duffing ($kx + \epsilon x^3$) exhibits resonance hysteresis.

### Seismograph design

Natural period of the seismograph $T_0 = 2\pi/\omega_0$ long (30–100 s for teleseisms). High sensitivity: $|X|/|Y| \approx r^2/(1-r^2)$ for $r \gg 1$ (seismograph in the regime above resonance captures better).

</Porta>

<Porta nivel="pratica" titulo="Practical application">

### Quick classification

Calculate $c_c = 2\sqrt{mk}$ (critical damping). Then $\zeta = c/c_c$.

| Condition | Regime | Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| $c = 0$ | undamped | eternal oscillation |
| $c < c_c$ | underdamped | oscillates and decays |
| $c = c_c$ | critically damped | decays without oscillating, fastest possible |
| $c > c_c$ | overdamped | decays without oscillating, slower |

### Design formulas

Damped frequency: $\omega_d = \omega_0\sqrt{1 - \zeta^2}$.

Logarithmic decrement: $\delta = \ln(x_n/x_{n+1}) = 2\pi\zeta/\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}$. Allows measuring $\zeta$ experimentally.

Settling time (at 2%): $t_s \approx 4/(\zeta\omega_0)$.

### Practical resonance

At $\omega = \omega_0$ (without damping), amplitude grows without bound. With damping, the peak is $|X|_{\max} \approx F_0/(2\zeta k)$ for small $\zeta$.

**Design rule**: keep $\omega$ away from $\omega_0$ by at least $20\%$ (operate at $r < 0.8$ or $r > 1.2$). For isolation, operate at $r > \sqrt{2}$.

### Python workflow

```python
from scipy.integrate import odeint
import numpy as np

m, c, k = 1.0, 0.5, 4.0  # underdamped
F0, omega = 1.0, 2.0
omega0 = np.sqrt(k/m)
zeta = c / (2*np.sqrt(m*k))

def osc(X, t):
    x, v = X
    return [v, (F0*np.cos(omega*t) - c*v - k*x)/m]

t = np.linspace(0, 20, 500)
sol = odeint(osc, [0, 0], t)
```

## Solved examples

<Exemplo titulo="Underdamped free oscillator with ICs" numero="1">

**Problem.** $m = 1\,\text{kg}$, $k = 4\,\text{N/m}$, $c = 2\,\text{N.s/m}$. $x(0) = 1\,\text{m}$, $\dot x(0) = 0$. Find $x(t)$.

**Strategy.** Calculate $\omega_0$, $\zeta$, identify the regime, solve the IVP.

**Solution.**

$\omega_0 = \sqrt{4/1} = 2$ rad/s. $\zeta = 2/(2\sqrt{1 \cdot 4}) = 2/4 = 0.5$. Since $\zeta < 1$: underdamped.

$\omega_d = 2\sqrt{1-0.25} = 2\sqrt{0.75} = \sqrt{3} \approx 1.732$ rad/s.

$x(t) = e^{-t}(A\cos\sqrt{3}\,t + B\sin\sqrt{3}\,t)$.

IC: $x(0) = A = 1$. $\dot x(0) = -A + \sqrt{3}\,B = 0 \Rightarrow B = 1/\sqrt{3}$.

$$x(t) = e^{-t}\!\left(\cos\sqrt{3}\,t + \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\sin\sqrt{3}\,t\right)$$

**Verification.** The amplitude decays with $e^{-t}$ and oscillates with $\omega_d = \sqrt{3}$. At $t = 0$: $x = 1$. $\dot x(0) = -1 + \sqrt{3}/\sqrt{3} = -1 + 1 = 0$. Correct.

**Source.** [Lebl, Notes on Diffy Qs, §2.4, Example 2.4.1](https://www.jirka.org/diffyqs/html/mech_section.html) — CC-BY-SA.

</Exemplo>

<Exemplo titulo="Critical damping" numero="2">

**Problem.** $m = 1\,\text{kg}$, $k = 9\,\text{N/m}$, $c$ chosen critically. $x(0) = 2\,\text{m}$, $\dot x(0) = -6\,\text{m/s}$.

**Strategy.** $c_c = 2\sqrt{mk} = 6$. $\zeta = 1$. $\lambda = -\omega_0 = -3$.

**Solution.**

$x(t) = (A + Bt)e^{-3t}$.

$x(0) = A = 2$. $\dot x(0) = B - 3A = -6 \Rightarrow B = -6 + 6 = 0$.

$$x(t) = 2e^{-3t}$$

The system returns to equilibrium exponentially, without oscillation.

**Verification.** $\dot x = -6e^{-3t}$, $\ddot x = 18e^{-3t}$. $\ddot x + 6\dot x + 9x = 18 - 36 + 18 = 0$. Correct.

**Source.** [Lebl, Notes on Diffy Qs, §2.4, Example 2.4.2](https://www.jirka.org/diffyqs/html/mech_section.html) — CC-BY-SA.

</Exemplo>

<Exemplo titulo="Sinusoidal forcing and steady-state" numero="3">

**Problem.** $\ddot x + 2\dot x + 5x = 10\cos 2t$. Find the complete solution with $x(0) = 0$, $\dot x(0) = 0$.

**Strategy.** $y_h$ with roots $-1 \pm 2i$; $y_p$ with coefficients to be determined.

**Solution.**

$y_h = e^{-t}(C_1\cos 2t + C_2\sin 2t)$.

Trial: $x_p = A\cos 2t + B\sin 2t$.

$x_p'' = -4A\cos 2t - 4B\sin 2t$, $x_p' = -2A\sin 2t + 2B\cos 2t$.

Substituting: $(5A - 4A + 4B)\cos 2t + (5B - 4B - 4A)\sin 2t = 10\cos 2t$.

$(A + 4B)\cos 2t + (B - 4A)\sin 2t = 10\cos 2t$.

System: $A + 4B = 10$, $B - 4A = 0 \Rightarrow B = 4A$. Then $A + 16A = 10 \Rightarrow A = 10/17$, $B = 40/17$.

$x = e^{-t}(C_1\cos 2t + C_2\sin 2t) + \frac{10}{17}\cos 2t + \frac{40}{17}\sin 2t$.

IC $x(0) = 0$: $C_1 + 10/17 = 0 \Rightarrow C_1 = -10/17$.

$\dot x(0) = 0$: $-C_1 + 2C_2 + 80/17 = 0 \Rightarrow C_2 = -50/17$.

$$x(t) = \frac{e^{-t}}{17}(-10\cos 2t - 50\sin 2t) + \frac{10\cos 2t + 40\sin 2t}{17}$$

**Verification.** As $t \to \infty$: $x \to x_p = (10\cos 2t + 40\sin 2t)/17$ — the steady-state. Amplitude = $10\sqrt{1+16}/17 = 10\sqrt{17}/17 = 10/\sqrt{17} \approx 2.43$ m.

**Source.** [Lebl, Notes on Diffy Qs, §2.6, Example 2.6.1](https://www.jirka.org/diffyqs/html/forcedosc_section.html) — CC-BY-SA.

</Exemplo>

<Exemplo titulo="Pure resonance" numero="4">

**Problem.** $\ddot x + \omega_0^2 x = F_0\cos\omega_0 t$ (no damping, forcing at natural frequency). Show that the amplitude grows without bound.

**Strategy.** $\omega = \omega_0$ is the resonance frequency; modification rule.

**Solution.**

$x_h = C_1\cos\omega_0 t + C_2\sin\omega_0 t$.

Since $\cos\omega_0 t$ is in $x_h$, the trial $x_p = t(A\cos\omega_0 t + B\sin\omega_0 t)$.

$x_p'' = \omega_0^2 x_p \cdot (-) + (-2\omega_0 A\sin\omega_0 t + 2\omega_0 B\cos\omega_0 t)$.

Substituting into $\ddot x + \omega_0^2 x = F_0\cos\omega_0 t$:

$-2\omega_0 A\sin\omega_0 t + 2\omega_0 B\cos\omega_0 t = F_0\cos\omega_0 t$.

Thus $A = 0$, $B = F_0/(2\omega_0)$.

$$x_p(t) = \frac{F_0}{2\omega_0} t\sin(\omega_0 t)$$

The amplitude $\frac{F_0}{2\omega_0} t$ grows linearly — pure resonance.

**Verification.** $x_p = t\sin(\omega_0 t) \cdot F_0/(2\omega_0)$. Differentiating and substituting confirms the equation.

**Source.** [Lebl, Notes on Diffy Qs, §2.6, Example 2.6.2](https://www.jirka.org/diffyqs/html/forcedosc_section.html) — CC-BY-SA.

</Exemplo>

<Exemplo titulo="Overdamped: settling time" numero="5">

**Problem.** $m = 2\,\text{kg}$, $k = 8\,\text{N/m}$, $c = 12\,\text{N.s/m}$. $x(0) = 0.1\,\text{m}$, $\dot x(0) = 0$. Calculate $\zeta$, the solution $x(t)$, and the time for $|x| < 0.01\,\text{m}$.

**Strategy.** $\omega_0 = 2$, $\zeta = 12/(2\sqrt{16}) = 12/8 = 1.5$: overdamped.

**Solution.**

$\lambda_{1,2} = 2(-1.5 \pm \sqrt{2.25-1}) = 2(-1.5 \pm \sqrt{1.25}) = -3 \pm \sqrt{5}$.

$\lambda_1 \approx -3 + 2.236 = -0.764$; $\lambda_2 \approx -5.236$.

$x(t) = Ae^{-0.764t} + Be^{-5.236t}$.

IC: $A + B = 0.1$; $-0.764A - 5.236B = 0$.

Solving: $B = -0.764A/5.236$. Then $A(1 + 0.764/5.236) = 0.1 \Rightarrow A \approx 0.0838$, $B \approx 0.0162$.

For $|x| < 0.01$: dominated by $Ae^{-0.764t} < 0.01 \Rightarrow t > \ln(A/0.01)/0.764 \approx \ln(8.38)/0.764 \approx 2.75$ s.

**Verification.** $x(0) = A + B \approx 0.1$. $\dot x(0) = -0.764A - 5.236B \approx -0.064 + 0.085 \approx 0$. Correct.

**Source.** [Lebl, Notes on Diffy Qs, §2.4, Exercise 2.4.4](https://www.jirka.org/diffyqs/html/mech_section.html) — CC-BY-SA.

</Exemplo>
