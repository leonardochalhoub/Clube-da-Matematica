### The SVD Theorem

<Definicao titulo="Singular Value Decomposition (SVD)">

For every <Eq>{`A \\in \\mathbb{R}^{m \\times n}`}</Eq>, there exist:

- <Eq>{`U \\in \\mathbb{R}^{m \\times m}`}</Eq> orthogonal (columns = **left singular vectors**),
- <Eq>{`V \\in \\mathbb{R}^{n \\times n}`}</Eq> orthogonal (columns = **right singular vectors**),
- <Eq>{`\\Sigma \\in \\mathbb{R}^{m \\times n}`}</Eq> with diagonal entries <Eq>{`\\sigma_1 \\geq \\sigma_2 \\geq \\cdots \\geq \\sigma_r > 0 = \\sigma_{r+1} = \\cdots`}</Eq>,

such that <Eq>{`A = U \\Sigma V^T`}</Eq>. The integer <Eq>{`r = \\text{rank}(A)`}</Eq>.

</Definicao>

> "Any matrix A with rank r can be written as a product $A = U \Sigma V^T$ where U is orthogonal ($m \times m$), $\Sigma$ is diagonal ($m \times n$, nonnegative entries decreasing), and V is orthogonal ($n \times n$). The diagonal entries $\sigma_1 \geq \cdots \geq \sigma_r > 0$ are the singular values of A." — [Understanding Linear Algebra, §6.3](https://understandinglinearalgebra.org/chap6.html)

<Definicao titulo="Compact SVD and rank-1 expansion">

The **compact (economic) SVD** retains only the <Eq>{`r`}</Eq> non-trivial columns:

<Equation explicacao="Expansion: A is a sum of r rank-1 matrices, weighted by singular values." latex="A = U_r \Sigma_r V_r^T = \sum_{i=1}^r \sigma_i\, u_i v_i^T" />

Each term <Eq>{`\\sigma_i u_i v_i^T`}</Eq> is a **rank-1 matrix** — outer product of a column vector and a row vector.

</Definicao>

> "Theorem (Existence of SVD). Every real matrix A has a singular value decomposition. The singular values are the positive square roots of the nonzero eigenvalues of $A^T A$." — [A First Course in Linear Algebra, §SVD](http://linear.ups.edu/html/section-SVD.html)

### Connection to eigenvalues

<Definicao titulo="SVD and the matrices A^T A and A A^T">

- <Eq>{`A^T A = V (\\Sigma^T \\Sigma) V^T`}</Eq>: the **eigenvalues** of <Eq>{`A^T A`}</Eq> are <Eq>{`\\sigma_i^2`}</Eq>, eigenvectors = columns of <Eq>{`V`}</Eq>.
- <Eq>{`A A^T = U (\\Sigma \\Sigma^T) U^T`}</Eq>: the **eigenvalues** of <Eq>{`A A^T`}</Eq> are the same <Eq>{`\\sigma_i^2`}</Eq>, eigenvectors = columns of <Eq>{`U`}</Eq>.
- Singular values are **always real and non-negative**, even if <Eq>{`A`}</Eq> has complex eigenvalues.

</Definicao>

### Eckart-Young Theorem

<Teorema titulo="Best rank-k approximation (Eckart-Young, 1936)">

The best rank-<Eq>{`k`}</Eq> approximation to <Eq>{`A`}</Eq> (in Frobenius norm and spectral norm) is:

<Equation explicacao="Truncating the SVD at the k largest singular values gives the best rank-k approximation in the least-squares sense." latex="A_k = \sum_{i=1}^k \sigma_i\, u_i v_i^T" />

Errors: <Eq>{`\\|A - A_k\\|_F^2 = \\sigma_{k+1}^2 + \\cdots + \\sigma_r^2`}</Eq>, <Eq>{`\\|A - A_k\\|_2 = \\sigma_{k+1}`}</Eq>.

</Teorema>

### Moore-Penrose Pseudoinverse

<Definicao titulo="Pseudoinverse via SVD">

<Equation explicacao="Inverts the positive singular values in place of Sigma; zeros the null ones. Solves min ||Ax - b|| with minimum-norm solution." latex="A^+ = V \Sigma^+ U^T" />

where <Eq>{`\\Sigma^+`}</Eq> is obtained by replacing each <Eq>{`\\sigma_i > 0`}</Eq> with <Eq>{`1/\\sigma_i`}</Eq> and transposing. The solution <Eq>{`x^* = A^+ b`}</Eq> minimizes <Eq>{`\\|Ax - b\\|`}</Eq> with smallest norm <Eq>{`\\|x\\|`}</Eq> among all minimizers.

</Definicao>

<Insight>

The **condition number** of <Eq>{`A`}</Eq> is <Eq>{`\\kappa_2(A) = \\sigma_1 / \\sigma_r`}</Eq>. A system <Eq>{`Ax = b`}</Eq> with large <Eq>{`\\kappa_2`}</Eq> is ill-conditioned: small errors in <Eq>{`b`}</Eq> produce large errors in <Eq>{`x`}</Eq>. SVD is the standard diagnostic tool for ill-conditioning.

</Insight>

<Porta nivel="5" titulo="For a 5-year-old">

Imagine you have a **colored photo** of a dog. The photo is full of colored dots — there are so many! SVD is a **magic trick for organizing**: it separates the photo into **layers of importance**.

The first layer captures the most important parts — the outline of the dog. The second captures slightly smaller details. And so on.

If you combine just the first most important layers, you can still recognize the dog — but using **much less memory**. That's how computers store smaller photos without losing too much quality.

</Porta>

<Porta nivel="10" titulo="For a 10-year-old">

Every matrix can be taken apart into three pieces: <Eq>{`A = U \\Sigma V^T`}</Eq>.

- <Eq>{`V^T`}</Eq> is a **rotation** of the input space.
- <Eq>{`\\Sigma`}</Eq> is a **stretching** — stretches or shrinks each direction by a number <Eq>{`\\sigma_i \\geq 0`}</Eq>.
- <Eq>{`U`}</Eq> is another **rotation** in the output space.

These numbers <Eq>{`\\sigma_i`}</Eq> — the **singular values** — tell you the importance of each direction. The largest <Eq>{`\\sigma_1`}</Eq> is the most important direction; the smallest is the least important.

**Compression trick**: in a photo of <Eq>{`1000 \\times 1000`}</Eq> pixels, keeping just the 50 largest singular values, you store <Eq>{`50 \\times 1000 + 50 + 50 \\times 1000 = 100050`}</Eq> numbers instead of 1 million — **10 times less**. The image still looks good.

</Porta>

<Porta nivel="15" titulo="For a 15-year-old teen">

SVD is the most important decomposition in applied linear algebra. It combines three properties that no other decomposition has at the same time: **generality** (works for any matrix), **orthogonality** (the bases <Eq>{`U, V`}</Eq> are orthonormal) and **uniqueness** (singular values are unique).

### Geometry

Every linear map <Eq>{`A: \\mathbb{R}^n \\to \\mathbb{R}^m`}</Eq> sends the unit sphere to an **ellipsoid**. The semi-axes of the ellipsoid have lengths <Eq>{`\\sigma_1 \\geq \\cdots \\geq \\sigma_r`}</Eq> and point in directions <Eq>{`u_1, \\ldots, u_r`}</Eq>.

- <Eq>{`V^T`}</Eq> rotates the input space (aligns the vectors <Eq>{`v_i`}</Eq> with the canonical axes).
- <Eq>{`\\Sigma`}</Eq> stretches each axis by <Eq>{`\\sigma_i`}</Eq>.
- <Eq>{`U`}</Eq> rotates the result into the output space.

### Comparison with diagonalization

| Aspect | Diagonalization | SVD |
|---|---|---|
| Applies to | Square diagonalizable matrices | Any matrix |
| Existence | Can fail | Always exists |
| Bases | <Eq>{`P, P^{-1}`}</Eq> (not orthogonal) | <Eq>{`U, V`}</Eq> orthogonal |
| Values | Eigenvalues (<Eq>{`\\mathbb{C}`}</Eq> in general) | Singular (<Eq>{`\\geq 0`}</Eq> real) |

### Immediate application: image compression

A grayscale image is a matrix <Eq>{`M \\in \\mathbb{R}^{m \\times n}`}</Eq>. Truncating the SVD at <Eq>{`k`}</Eq> terms:

- Storing <Eq>{`A_k`}</Eq> requires <Eq>{`k(m + n + 1)`}</Eq> numbers instead of <Eq>{`mn`}</Eq>.
- Relative Frobenius error: <Eq>{`\\|A - A_k\\|_F / \\|A\\|_F = \\sqrt{\\sum_{i>k} \\sigma_i^2} / \\sqrt{\\sum_i \\sigma_i^2}`}</Eq>.
